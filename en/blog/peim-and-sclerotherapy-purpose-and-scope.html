PEIM: what should be agreed about aftercare and follow-up?

PEIM refers to an injectable aesthetic procedure for small visible vessels. Sclerotherapy also involves injectable agents, but professional references may distinguish it by clinical purpose. Clarifying the goal and who assesses the case matters before comparing the terms.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; confirm the local protocol

A technical similarity does not make the purpose the same

Both terms may appear in conversations about visible vessels and injections, but they should not be treated as synonyms without clarifying the concern, purpose, and assessment. Visible small vessels are not, by themselves, a diagnosis of venous disease; symptoms or larger veins may require appropriate health assessment.

Cofen Opinion No. 3/2026 describes PEIM as an aesthetic-purpose procedure and distinguishes sclerotherapy as treatment for a pathological condition within its analysis of nursing scope. This is a scope document for Nursing — it does not define the competence of other professions or replace the rules of the relevant professional council.

Questions to ask before considering a procedure

  • What is the exact name of the proposed procedure and what goal is intended?
  • Did the assessment identify an aesthetic concern only, or are there symptoms that need vascular investigation?
  • Who will assess and perform the procedure, and what training and professional scope applies to that activity?
  • Which product will be used, what is its purpose and regulatory status, and which risks and alternatives will be explained?
  • What written care instructions and contact channel will be available afterward?

Do not let similarities in instruments, treatment area, or route of administration replace an explanation of indication and professional qualifications.

When a concern may be more than cosmetic

Persistent pain, swelling, skin changes, a wound, bleeding, or a marked change in one leg warrant clinical assessment; a page about PEIM cannot determine the cause or indicate a test. If the team suspects venous disease, assessment should follow the appropriate professional and context instead of assuming an aesthetic procedure addresses the concern.

Product checks and professional scope are different questions

Anvisa advises checking the product and the establishment’s sanitary conditions and explains that it does not regulate each profession’s practice. So confirming a product or finding a procedure described online does not, by itself, answer who may perform it in each professional category. For qualification questions, consult the relevant professional council and current rules.

This article does not assess the legality of an individual case, determine which professional may perform a specific procedure, or describe this clinic’s product or protocol.
