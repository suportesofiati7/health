IPL and laser: differences, goals and assessment

IPL and laser are different technologies. Learn what changes and which questions help guide an individual assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Start with the context

Start with the type of light

Intense pulsed light (IPL) and laser are not interchangeable terms. The FDA describes IPL as a category distinct from lasers for purposes of its radiation-emitting product rules. In simple terms, a laser emits light within a narrower wavelength range; IPL delivers pulses across a broader range that may be filtered by the device. That distinction alone does not tell us which technology is appropriate for a person.

02 · The technique comes after the question

A platform name does not define a procedure

A platform may include different technologies or applicators. Selection depends on the equipment and applicator actually available, the intended target, the skin assessment and the protocol authorized for that device. A platform’s brand name alone does not identify which light will be used or establish that a particular concern can be treated.

03 · What is useful to observe

What to bring to an assessment

Explain your main concern and how long you have noticed it. Mention recent sun exposure or tanning, irritation, products and procedures used on the area, and previous reactions. These details help the professional decide whether to assess further, wait or consider another option; they do not let a person determine their own indication.

04 · Safety can include waiting

Selection also depends on limits and timing

There is no universal IPL or laser setting for every skin, target or device. “Stronger” should not be assumed to mean better. Ask which device and applicator are being considered, what realistic goal guides the choice, what alternatives exist, and what limitations and possible adverse effects should be understood. Specific preparation and aftercare instructions should come from the device protocol and the professional who assessed you.

05 · How a consultation clarifies the next step

Useful questions before deciding

You might ask: “Is this IPL or laser?”, “Which applicator is being considered and for what goal?”, “What may change, and what cannot be promised?”, “What could make it better to wait?”, and “Which aftercare and warning signs should I know about?”. Answers should match the device and your case, without turning general information into a diagnosis or guarantee.
