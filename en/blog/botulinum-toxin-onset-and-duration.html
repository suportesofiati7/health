Botulinum toxin: when does it start and how long does it last?

Onset and duration vary by product, area, outcome measured and individual. Learn how to interpret timelines without treating them as a promise.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Clinical information; confirm the product and plan with your practitioner.

“Onset” and “duration” can mean different things

“It has started working” may refer to an early change, the effect noticed at a visit, or an outcome measured in a study. Those milestones are not interchangeable. The treated area, movement assessed, product and measurement method all shape the response.

A number seen online or in a label is therefore not an individual countdown. Keep the treatment date and agree with the practitioner on when reassessment is appropriate.

Labels describe specific studies, not a personal guarantee

As context, the US Dysport label reports reduction in glabellar-line severity for up to four months in specific studies and reports an assessment at Day 30. The Botox Cosmetic label presents data by indication and its own time points. This does not establish that one product lasts longer: populations, endpoints, doses and study designs differ.

Products have product-specific units and instructions. Units should not be converted, and a studied indication should not be extrapolated to a different facial area.

Why can people perceive results differently?

Movement strength and pattern, anatomy, the agreed goal and individual response may affect what someone notices. It also matters whether comparison is made at rest or during expression and whether photographs were taken under similar conditions.

If the effect feels weaker, uneven or different from expectations, note the date, what changed and your questions. Do not try to compensate on your own or use someone else’s experience to set a repeat treatment.

Questions to ask before treatment and at follow-up

Ask which medicine will be used, which indication is being considered, what follow-up milestone the practitioner recommends and what changes should prompt earlier contact. Request the Brazilian professional leaflet for the exact product and ask how the result will be assessed.

The clinic website does not publicly identify the brand used or a specific follow-up timeline. Confirm those details directly; a universal repeat interval should not be suggested.

Sources and limits

Official US labels illustrate results for specific products and studied indications. They do not identify the product used at this clinic, establish Brazilian instructions, or predict an individual result. Product units are not interchangeable.

  • FDA: Botox Cosmetic prescribing information (2019)

These examples should not be used to choose a product, dose or repeat interval. Confirm current Brazilian package information for the exact medicine and indication with the professional responsible.
