A changing skin spot: when to seek assessment before pigment treatment

A new or changing skin spot should not be assumed to be a cosmetic pigment concern. Learn when to pause a pigment-treatment decision and seek dermatology assessment.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; dermatology assessment when indicated

01 · Not every spot is cosmetic hyperpigmentation

Appearance alone cannot establish the cause

Flat marks can have different causes, and a mole or another pigmented lesion can resemble a cosmetic concern. A webpage or photograph cannot diagnose it. Before planning pigment treatment, find out whether the lesion has been assessed by a medical professional.

Brazil’s Ministry of Health lists itchy, scaly or bleeding spots, marks that change in size, shape or colour, and sores that do not heal among signs that deserve attention. This does not mean every change is cancer; it means the cause should not be assumed without assessment.

02 · Change over time deserves attention

Compare the spot with itself and with other spots

The ABCDE rule is an educational tool for noticing signs that warrant assessment: asymmetry, irregular borders, varied colour, diameter and evolution. The AAD notes that a concerning lesion can be smaller than six millimetres; size alone does not rule out a problem.

A spot that looks different from others, a new lesion, or an area that grows, changes, itches or bleeds should be assessed by a dermatologist. ABCDE can help someone decide when to seek care, but it cannot confirm or rule out a diagnosis.

03 · Why pause an aesthetic decision

Clarify the lesion first; discuss pigment care afterward

Pigment procedures are planned for concerns assessed as appropriate for that care. They do not replace a dermatology examination, dermoscopy or biopsy when a physician considers those appropriate. Do not apply a bleaching product or procedure to a changing lesion before its nature is clarified.

If an area is new or evolving, tell the practitioner who would perform the procedure and seek dermatology assessment before treating that area. An aesthetic team can record the concern and direct the person to appropriate medical care, but should not classify a lesion from a photograph or from the ABCDE rule.

04 · Preparing for a medical appointment

Bring a simple history without trying to self-diagnose

Note when you first noticed the lesion and any changes in size, shape, colour, texture, itching, bleeding or healing. Earlier photographs taken in similar conditions may help describe its evolution, but they do not replace an in-person examination.

Mention relevant personal or family history of skin cancer and any treatment already performed on the area. Avoid scraping, filing, cauterising or trying to bleach the lesion before it is assessed. After the medical review, aesthetic questions can be discussed separately in light of the diagnosis and guidance received.
