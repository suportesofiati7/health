Laser hair removal: maintenance, regrowth and when to reassess

A practical guide to ongoing hair reduction, visible regrowth and deciding whether another assessment or session makes sense.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Evidence-informed reading

Guidance and outcomes depend on the individual assessment, device and current treatment plan. This article does not prescribe a maintenance interval or assume that a particular device is used.

01 · Long-term reduction is not a promise of permanent absence

What maintenance means in practice

After an initial series, some people notice slower growth, lower density or finer hairs; others see some visible regrowth over time. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that maintenance treatments may be needed to keep an area free of hair. This does not mean everyone needs repeat sessions on the same schedule, or that one session failing to remove every hair is a treatment failure. Response and duration vary with area, hair, skin, device and individual factors.

02 · Visible regrowth can have more than one explanation

Hair cycle, incomplete response and change over time

Different hairs may be in different growth phases when an area is treated, and not every hair responds in the same way. Later, a person may notice residual hairs, areas with renewed growth or new hairs. Looking at the skin alone cannot tell whether these are follicles that did not respond, newly visible hairs or a change related to biological factors. Review the interval and whether to continue or stop with the practitioner; do not decide from the calendar alone.

03 · Compare trends, not a single week

How to track change without turning photos into a promise

If you choose to document progress, use similar lighting, distance and intervals between photographs; note the area, when growth appeared and whether amount, thickness or distribution changed. Comparing a photo taken just after shaving with one from a different growth phase can mislead. Images help organize a discussion, but they cannot by themselves measure responsive follicles or predict the result of another session.

04 · When to request a new assessment

A change may call for another question or a different referral

Talk with the practitioner before automatically repeating if the pattern changed, growth is concentrated in one area, you had irritation, a burn or pigment change, or your skin is tanned or sensitised. New or progressive facial hair growth—especially alongside other body or menstrual changes—may warrant a health assessment to investigate the cause. Laser reduces treated hair; it does not identify or correct a hormonal cause. Our guide to facial hair and hormonal context explores this distinction further.

05 · Reassessment also means updating your history

What to share before a maintenance session

Mention new medicines, health changes, recent tanning, a prior reaction and any other treatment in the area. Do not stop medicine or change care on your own. The AAD recommends discussing medicines, tanning, scarring tendency and other factors before treatment. The practitioner should confirm the current device and proposal; a platform’s brand name is not a substitute for checking the actual equipment and protocol used.

06 · Useful questions before booking

The next session should have a clear reason

Ask: What changed since the last assessment? What is this session meant to do? Does the current pattern suggest maintenance or another investigation? How was the interval chosen for this area? Which skin change should prompt contact? Is the device and plan still appropriate for my skin tone, hair and history? Is there a reason to wait or not repeat now? A responsible answer may be to adjust the plan, investigate a new factor or recommend waiting.
