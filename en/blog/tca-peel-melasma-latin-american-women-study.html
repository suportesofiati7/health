TCA peel and melasma: what did a study in Latin American women find?

A split-face trial evaluated superficial TCA as an addition to topical medicines for melasma. Read the findings and the study’s limits.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational review of clinical evidence

01 · The trial’s question

TCA was studied as an addition to a topical plan

A prospective, randomised split-face trial examined Latin American women with melasma. Both sides received topical hydroquinone and tretinoin; one side also received four superficial trichloroacetic acid (TCA) peels, given monthly. This design helps ask whether peeling added anything to that specific plan.

The study did not test TCA alone and did not evaluate every type of melasma, formula, depth or patient profile. Its findings cannot make “TCA peel” a uniform intervention.

02 · What the results suggested

Some measures favoured the peeled side, but not all

Thirty-three participants completed 16 weeks of follow-up. The authors reported greater improvement on the TCA side in some clinical severity scales compared with the side receiving only the study’s topical medicines. An objective pigmentation measure, however, improved more on the side without peeling. This mixed picture matters: the findings cannot be summarised as guaranteed lightening or superiority across every outcome.

No severe adverse events were observed in this group, but acne flares, redness, itching, irritation, minor erosions, burning and herpes reactivation occurred. A sample of 33 cannot rule out less common events or establish individual risk.

03 · Why the findings may not apply to your situation

Participants, combination and duration matter

Participants had moderate-to-severe bilateral facial melasma; 25 had Fitzpatrick skin type IV and eight type III. The protocol included topical medicines and planned pauses around peels. Pregnancy and breastfeeding were among the exclusion criteria. These details are not advice to start, stop or combine medicines on your own.

Follow-up ended at 16 weeks. The trial does not establish long-term maintenance, response to another formula, safety of a local protocol or what happens without the topical treatments used in the study. The research can inform a clinical discussion, not function as a prescription.

04 · Questions before deciding

Confirm the diagnosis and the full plan

  • Has the spot been assessed and identified as melasma?
  • Which agent, formulation and depth are actually being proposed?
  • Did the cited study use the same combination, population and follow-up?
  • Which benefits, uncertainties and adverse events should be discussed?
  • How does the plan address recurrence, sun protection and alternatives?

The formula and protocol for this clinic’s TCA peel need to be confirmed directly. Also see the professional pigmentation protocol and the difference between melasma and other marks.

05 · Source and limits

Published clinical trial

This is a small, 16-week trial of superficial TCA added to topical hydroquinone and tretinoin. It does not confirm this clinic’s protocol or show that the combination is appropriate for everyone.
