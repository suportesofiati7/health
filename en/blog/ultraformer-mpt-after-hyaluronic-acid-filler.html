Ultraformer MPT after dermal filler: is there a universal waiting interval?

The timing question depends on the filler, its location and depth, and the exact ultrasound device. One small study offers useful context, but it did not test facial Ultraformer MPT.

Start with the filler record and treatment area

Before considering focused ultrasound, tell the practitioner what filler was used, when and where it was placed, and whether you have a product card or procedure record. Ask whether the planned ultrasound area overlaps the filler and which handpiece and device instructions apply. “Hyaluronic acid filler” covers products and placement depths that are not interchangeable.

What one small study examined

A 2022 pilot study involved 14 volunteers. Researchers placed small amounts of hyaluronic acid in four abdominal skin areas and used a 1.5 mm microfocused-ultrasound transducer at one hour, 14 days, or 28 days after injection. One participant’s histology was excluded for slide quality. At day 56, the measured filler grade had decreased from baseline in 7 of 13 areas treated at one hour and 6 of 13 treated at day 14; the day-28 comparison was not statistically significant. No inflammation or granuloma was observed in that study.

This was a small study of intradermal abdominal filler and a particular ultrasound transducer. It did not test facial filler at different depths, the Ultraformer MPT clinic device, or every cartridge and treatment plan. A nonsignificant result at day 28 is not proof of a universal safe date.

Why the study does not set a waiting rule

Filler formulation, cross-linking, injection plane, facial location, ultrasound device, transducer, energy settings, and treatment target can differ. The study authors’ conclusion applies to their protocol and should not be converted into an instruction for another device or person. It also does not answer whether a treatment could change the result a patient wants to preserve.

There is not enough evidence here to name one interval for everyone. The practitioner must check the exact filler history against the current instructions for the device actually used and the proposed area.

Questions to take to the assessment

  • What filler product and placement area should I report, and what record would help?
  • Does the planned treatment overlap the filler, and what cartridge and depth are being considered?
  • What do the current device instructions and clinical evidence say for this exact situation?
  • Would waiting, changing the target area, or choosing another option better fit my goal?
  • Who should I contact if I notice an unexpected change after treatment?

Do not use a date from a small study, a different ultrasound platform, or an online rule to schedule a procedure by yourself. The exact clinic device and protocol require practitioner confirmation.

Source and limits

  • Vachiramon et al. (2023), clinicopathological pilot study of microfocused ultrasound after hyaluronic acid filler.
  • CLASSYS Ultraformer MPT overview; confirms the platform uses multiple cartridges and modes but does not set patient-specific timing.

The pilot cannot establish a universal interval, facial outcome, or local protocol. Confirm the filler record, device instructions, and proposed treatment with the practitioner.
