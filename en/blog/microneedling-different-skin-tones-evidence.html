JOURNAL / Microneedling and pigmentation

Microneedling across skin tones: what does the evidence say?

The literature does not turn skin tone into an automatic answer about safety or contraindication. See what studies actually examined and which questions help individualise the discussion.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

There is no single outcome for every skin tone

Microneedling punctures the skin; response may depend on the indication, device and protocol, current skin condition and individual factors. The useful question is not simply “is it safe for dark skin?” but which concern is being assessed, what pigmentation risks matter and which evidence matches the technique being proposed.

Studies answer specific questions

A 2018 study followed 39 participants with darker skin, classified by the authors as phototypes III to V, who received microneedling for acne scars associated with pigmentation. The authors reported improvement on the scales used and did not observe worsened pigmentation in that group. This describes that population, indication and protocol; it cannot guarantee the same response for another person or device.

A safety systematic review covering 85 articles also found reports of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation and advised caution for darker skin. Reviews and studies may differ in indication, technique, follow-up and quality. They therefore support neither a universal promise nor an automatic ban based only on skin tone.

History of dark marks and inflammation matters too

Tell the practitioner if acne, insect bites, burns, wounds or other procedures have left persistent marks; mention active irritation or inflammation and recent products or treatments. This information helps the team understand your history. Do not use a phototype scale or photograph to estimate your personal risk on your own.

Questions to ask about the proposal

  • What is the goal of this technique in my case, and what assessment supports the proposal?
  • Which studies match the indication, device and protocol being considered?
  • How do my history of pigmentation, healing and irritation affect the decision?
  • Which pigmentation changes will be discussed as risks, and how will follow-up work?
  • Which products will be used and by which route? Does the product documentation support that use?
  • What alternatives exist, including postponing or not having the procedure?

Do not stop medication or start products on your own to “prepare” your skin. Ask the practitioner for individual written instructions.

Microneedling is not an automatic treatment for dark marks

A dark mark can have different causes, and microneedling should not be presented as a universal solution for pigmentation. These materials do not replace dermatology assessment when the cause needs investigation.
