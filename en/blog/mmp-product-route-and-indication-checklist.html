MMP: how to confirm the product, route and indication before treatment

The term MMP does not identify the product or how it will be used. Learn what to ask and how to check its regulatory status without assuming the clinic’s protocol.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational guide; confirm actual product and protocol

The technique name does not identify what will be used

MMP stands for microinfusion of medicines into the skin, but the public service page does not identify the substance, product, device or indication planned for an individual. The procedure name alone does not tell you whether a medicine, medical device or another step is involved.

Before deciding, ask for the product’s full name and regulatory category, the proposed purpose, how it will be used and which professional will perform each step. If the answer is “an active” or “a cocktail,” ask for its full identity and supporting documentation.

Check that category, label and instructions match

Anvisa states that cosmetics are for external use and cannot be regularised for injection. When a product is introduced by injection or microneedling, it penetrates the skin; the category and authorised purpose must be checked for the exact product. Without knowing the substance and route, this does not establish what happens in a specific appointment.

Ask to see the package and note the name, manufacturer and registration or process number. In Anvisa’s regularised-product search, select the appropriate category and compare the listing with the label. An “active” status indicates current regularisation, but does not by itself prove the product, route and indication are appropriate for every procedure or person.

The route and indication must match the evidence

Ask whether the product will be applied externally or introduced into the skin, which indication is being considered, and what evidence supports that exact product, route and goal. Evidence for a topical cosmetic, another device or another indication does not automatically establish use by microinfusion.

Also ask about alternatives, risks and how the product, lot and expiry date will be recorded. The practitioner should explain the plan in clear language before consent.

Product regularisation and professional authorisation are separate checks

Anvisa regulates products and sanitary aspects; the Agency also notes that professional practice and each category’s scope are determined by the relevant rules and professional councils. A database search alone does not confirm that any practitioner may perform any technique.

The service page identifies Franciele as a biomedical professional, aesthetician and cosmetologist, but does not publicly identify the product or proposed protocol. The substance, route or regulatory status of an individual treatment cannot be inferred from the term MMP. If these points are unclear, ask for clarification before consenting.
