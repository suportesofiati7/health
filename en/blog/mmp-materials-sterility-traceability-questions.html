MMP: questions about materials, sterility and traceability

Learn what to clarify about products, materials and records before deciding—without assuming how the procedure is performed at the clinic.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

The name MMP does not explain every material involved

The acronym for “microinfusion of medication into the skin” does not, by itself, identify the device, parts that contact the skin, any product used, intended route or how each item is processed. A service description also does not confirm the details of an individual session. Ask for a clear explanation of what will be used and why before consenting.

Understand what is single-use and what is reprocessed

Disposable items should be used according to their labels and the manufacturer’s directions and not reused when designated for single use. For reusable components, Anvisa says equipment, materials and utensils should undergo cleaning, disinfection or sterilisation after each use, as required. The exact process depends on the item and applicable sanitary rules; the technique’s name alone cannot confirm it.

You can ask which parts are discarded after the session and how reusable components are processed. A direct question is not an accusation, and observing the room does not verify internal procedures.

Product and batch details help clarify what was used

Before a procedure, ask for the product’s full name, manufacturer, purpose, regulatory category and expiry date. You can also ask whether product and batch details will be recorded in your chart or treatment record. Anvisa recommends that people know which product is being used and check its status through official channels. If a proposal involves delivery by an invasive route, the product category and directions must match that purpose; cosmetics are intended for external use.

A product’s registration does not, by itself, prove that it is appropriate for every indication, route or person. That requires the relevant documentation and an explanation from the responsible practitioner.

The setting and appointment are part of the conversation

Anvisa advises people to consider the establishment’s general hygiene and maintenance, and explains that reusable materials need appropriate processing. If you do not understand how items will be prepared, ask before the procedure starts. If the answer does not clarify what will be used, how it will be processed and how details will be recorded, you can postpone your decision until you have that information.

Questions to ask before consenting

  • What is the device model, and which parts will contact my skin?
  • Which items are disposable and which are reused?
  • How are reusable components cleaned, disinfected or sterilised, as required by the manufacturer and applicable rules?
  • Which product will be used, for what purpose and by which route?
  • Can I check the package, manufacturer, category, batch and expiry date?
  • Which product and procedure details will be recorded for follow-up?
  • Whom should I contact if I have a concern afterwards?

What this guide does not verify

Anvisa’s public guidance helps frame general questions; it does not verify this clinic’s equipment, product, material processing or protocol. It also does not determine an individual indication, professional scope or expected outcome. Those details should be confirmed by the practitioner before a decision.
