Laser hair removal over moles: what should be assessed first?

Pigmented nevi can change appearance after exposure to hair-removal laser. Tell the team about moles in the area and ask for assessment of any new or changing lesion.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Why mention moles before a session

Energy used to reduce hair can also interact with pigment in melanocytic lesions. Prospective studies and a systematic review have recorded dermoscopic or clinical changes in some nevi after photoepilation. Such changes do not, by themselves, mean a lesion has become cancer, but they may make later comparison with its earlier appearance more difficult.

Laser guidelines for pigmented lesions advise against intentionally treating pigmented melanocytic nevi without specialist assessment. That is different from proving that a hair-removal session causes melanoma; the available research does not establish that conclusion.

What to tell the treatment team

  • Whether moles or dark spots are within the planned area.
  • Whether a lesion is new, changing in size, shape or color, itchy, bleeding or unlike the others.
  • Any relevant personal or family history of skin cancer or atypical nevi.
  • Whether the area has prior photographs or dermatologic assessment.

Do not cover, shave, lighten or try to remove a mole yourself to allow a session. The practitioner should decide how to proceed; new or changing lesions should be assessed by a qualified health professional before cosmetic treatment.

If a mole changes after laser

Tell the team that performed the procedure and seek dermatologic assessment, especially if the change persists or progresses. Share the session date and, if possible, earlier records. Do not conclude remotely that the change is benign or that laser caused cancer; it needs clinical examination in context.

Questions before deciding

  • How does the team identify and avoid intentional exposure of pigmented lesions?
  • Does a specific mole need dermatologic assessment before treating nearby hair?
  • How can the area be documented so future changes can be compared?
  • Which professional should be contacted if a lesion changes after a session?

References and limits
