Scalp mesotherapy: questions about risks and alternatives

Before comparing a mesotherapy proposal, understand exactly which product, route, goal and evidence are being discussed.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

The procedure name does not describe the full proposal

“Scalp mesotherapy” does not, by itself, identify the substance, concentration, route and depth of delivery, proposed goal or evidence for that use. These details may differ between techniques. This article does not confirm the product or protocol available at the clinic; those details should be explained before any decision.

Start with the cause of hair loss or thinning

Shedding, reduced density and hair breakage can have different causes. A dermatology assessment generally considers the history, scalp and observed pattern; tests may be requested when the clinical question calls for them. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that identifying the cause guides care and that no one treatment works for everyone. A cosmetic procedure should not replace that evaluation when it is needed.

If the change is recent, marked, patchy, painful or inflamed, or continues to progress, seek appropriate health assessment before choosing a procedure. A photograph or online article cannot establish the cause.

Match evidence to the exact intervention

Studies of scalp mesotherapy examine different substances, techniques, diagnoses and outcome measures. A systematic review does not turn those varied protocols into proof that any “cocktail” is effective, or confirm that a study applies to a particular product or route. To interpret the evidence, compare the ingredient, formulation, route, studied population, outcome and follow-up period with the proposal being discussed.

Be cautious about conclusions based only on a plausible mechanism, a testimonial, photographs that were not taken under comparable conditions, or a list of ingredients without a clear indication and supporting data.

The product, regulatory status and route need clarification

Brazil’s Anvisa says that aesthetic products intended for injection cannot be regularised as cosmetics: the category must match the authorised purpose and way of use. This does not establish the status of a particular product, whether a technique is appropriate, or the professional scope that applies to a case. Ask for the product’s full name, manufacturer, category, registration number and intended directions, and check official information when needed.

Packaging, the phrase “professional use” or a general answer is not a substitute for checking the category and route. Do not inject cosmetics or use them with a technique that goes beyond the labelled directions.

Questions about risks, alternatives and follow-up

  • What concern is being evaluated, and why was this option proposed?
  • What is the product’s name, composition and regulatory category?
  • Which route will be used, and is it included in the authorised directions?
  • Which studies match this product, route, indication and patient profile?
  • Which unwanted effects are known, and what personal factors affect the assessment?
  • What alternatives exist, including investigating the cause or not proceeding?
  • How will changes be followed, and whom should I contact if I am concerned?

A responsible discussion should explain possible benefits, uncertainty, risks, alternatives and what has not been confirmed. You can ask for time to review the information before consenting.

An informed next step

Bring your questions to an assessment and ask for the proposal to be explained in clear language, including what is not known. A decision should consider the cause of the concern, the product documentation and available alternatives—not only the procedure name.
