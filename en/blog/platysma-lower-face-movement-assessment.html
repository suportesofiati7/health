Notice when the concern appears

A vertical neck band or a change in the jawline may become more visible during a particular expression, speech, or neck movement; other concerns remain similar when the face is relaxed. These observations are useful descriptions for a consultation, not a way to diagnose which structure is responsible. Lighting, posture, camera angle, and expression can also change how contours look in photographs.

If you choose to document a concern, describe the movement and setting consistently. You do not need to repeatedly provoke a movement or record yourself if that feels uncomfortable.

What the platysma trial studied

A phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial studied a specific onabotulinumtoxinA product in adults with moderate-to-severe platysma prominence. Its outcomes included clinician-rated prominence and participant reports. This provides evidence about that study population, product, and measured outcome; it does not show that every lower-face concern is caused by the platysma or that the same approach fits every person.

The study was industry-associated and authors disclosed financial relationships. Those details belong in a balanced reading of the results. Product authorization and labeling also differ by country; a US indication does not establish local availability, suitability, or a clinic protocol in Brazil.

Questions that make the assessment clearer

  • Does the feature change with a specific expression or remain at rest?
  • What outcome is being discussed: visible bands, jawline appearance, movement, or something else?
  • What findings support the proposed explanation, and what other causes or options were considered?
  • Which product-specific evidence and local professional rules apply?
  • What are the expected limits, possible effects on function, and follow-up plan?

Assessment should not be reduced to a photo, a fixed movement test, or an injection map. This guide does not identify treatment or provide injection instructions.

Sources and limits

  • Fabi et al. (2025), phase 3 trial of onabotulinumtoxinA for platysma prominence.
  • De Boulle et al. (2025), phase 3 study of neck and jawline aesthetics and platysma contraction effects.
  • US FDA BOTOX Cosmetic prescribing information (2024); country- and product-specific labeling.

The precise service, product and local protocol need practitioner confirmation. These sources do not determine an individual diagnosis or treatment plan.
