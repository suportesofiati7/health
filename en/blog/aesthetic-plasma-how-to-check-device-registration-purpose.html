Aesthetic plasma: how to check the device, registration and purpose

“Plasma” alone does not identify a device or its intended use. Learn what to confirm in Anvisa records and device documentation before comparing procedures.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; confirm modality and device

01 · Start with the device, not the nickname

“Plasma” can describe different technologies

In aesthetic discussions, “plasma” can refer to different modalities and devices. A popular name, photograph or outcome claim does not identify the manufacturer, model, stated purpose or instructions for use. Those distinctions matter because evidence and warnings for one device cannot automatically be transferred to another.

The local service page does not publicly identify its manufacturer and model. Before interpreting indications, safety or outcomes, ask the clinic for these details and the exact modality being considered.

02 · What an Anvisa search can confirm

Check that the record matches the exact product

Anvisa provides public searches for health products. Its guidance says searches can use a product or brand name, the responsible company, registration number or process number; the result shows the product’s status. Compare those details with the identification of the device being presented instead of searching only for the word “plasma”.

An active or valid status is information about that product’s regularisation. By itself, it does not prove that every advertised aesthetic use is appropriate for an individual, that devices with similar names are equivalent, or that any professional may operate it.

03 · Regularisation, intended use and professional qualification are different questions

A database search does not replace the manual or professional rules

Ask for the trade name and model, manufacturer and the regularisation number provided by the clinic. Then check whether the manufacturer’s stated purpose and warnings match what is proposed. Do not assume that a product listed as a medical device is cleared for every use described in advertising.

Anvisa distinguishes product and service sanitary regulation from rules governing professional practice, which involve professional councils and applicable legislation. Also ask who will perform the procedure, what professional training and authorisation apply, and whether the establishment meets local sanitary requirements. Do not infer these answers from the device record alone.

04 · Useful questions before deciding

Use the documentation to clarify what is being offered

You can ask: which plasma modality and exact device model are proposed? What purpose does the manufacturer state? Where can I see the instructions for use and regularisation number? Does the Anvisa record match the same manufacturer and model? Who will operate the device and which qualification rules apply? What risks, alternatives and limits have been explained for my concern?

If the information does not clearly identify the device or connect it to its documentation, pause the decision until you receive clarification. This does not diagnose or declare a service irregular; it simply avoids comparing different technologies as if they were the same.

05 · References and limits

References and limits

Anvisa guidance helps locate regulatory information, but it does not by itself confirm individual suitability, efficacy, safety for a person or professional qualification. The clinic’s device model is not identified on this page; the practitioner must verify these details before making device-specific claims.
