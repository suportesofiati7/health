Light for pigment or visible vessels: why does the target change assessment?

Pigmented marks and redness can have different causes. Learn why identifying the target and modality matters before considering laser or light.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Pigmented marks and redness are not the same target

A dark area may be pigmentation, a mark after inflammation, or another lesion; redness can involve visible vessels, inflammation, or conditions such as rosacea. Appearance alone does not confirm the cause. The AAD cautions that a spot that looks like an “age spot” may be another condition and should be assessed before attempts to fade it.

The modality matters too

“Light” does not identify one technology. Lasers and intense pulsed light have different properties; within each category, device, applicator and indication affect which evidence applies. The AAD describes laser or light as possibilities for certain redness and visible-vessel concerns, but that does not mean every mark or redness will respond, and it does not identify the configuration available on this clinic’s Harmony platform.

Questions to ask before deciding

Ask which target the practitioner is assessing — pigment, vessels or another concern — and how the cause was determined. Confirm the exact device model, applicator, intended purpose and whether the indication matches the manufacturer’s instructions. Ask about alternatives, limits, possible effects and written aftercare; do not compare outcomes from different devices as though they were the same method.

When medical assessment comes first

A new spot that grows, changes colour or shape, bleeds or does not heal should be assessed by a dermatologist before cosmetic attempts to fade it. Persistent redness with symptoms may also need investigation. This guide does not diagnose skin or confirm which applicators or indications are available at the local practice.
