Ultraformer MPT: when should you assess results?

There is no single day that applies to every person, area or goal. Ultraformer MPT studies assess specific measures at specific timepoints and protocols; they help explain how outcomes were evaluated, but do not promise the same timeline for another appointment.

When discussing timing

Define what will be followed

  1. Which goal and area are being assessed?
  2. What measure or change will be observed?
  3. When and by whom will reassessment take place?

What does “result” mean?

The answer depends on the goal. A laxity measure, a photograph, a volume change, a person’s perception and satisfaction are different outcomes. An observation immediately after a session does not automatically equal a lasting change, and a study image cannot predict how someone else will respond.

Before asking “when will it show?”, clarify which change is sought, how it will be assessed and what interval makes sense for that goal. Also ask which modality and handpiece are planned: the Ultraformer MPT name alone does not describe every detail of a treatment plan.

What one MPT study assessed at 12 weeks

A prospective cohort study published in 2025 followed 34 Korean women with mild to moderate eyelid laxity after one HIFU session using Ultraformer MPT. Researchers assessed the periocular area at 12 weeks using practitioner- and participant-rated improvement scales and standardized photographic measurements. The study had no comparator group and short follow-up; one listed affiliation is the manufacturer, Classys. This informs that area, population, configuration and protocol, but does not establish when another area or goal should be assessed or predict an individual result.

A case report on lower-face volume reduction recorded measurements at 60 and 120 days for one patient. A case can generate a hypothesis and show how follow-up was done; it cannot estimate the frequency or size of response in other people.

Why these timelines cannot be universalized

Protocols differ in goal, area, ultrasound type, handpiece, depth, number of sessions, measurements and follow-up length. A 2023 systematic review of microfocused ultrasound studies found variation in outcomes and assessment timepoints; longer follow-up data were limited. The review predates later research, but still illustrates why a broad technology name is not enough to set a personal timeline.

An assessment at 12 weeks in a periocular cohort is not a universal recommendation for cheeks, neck or another indication. Likewise, a volumetric measurement from a single case does not establish benefit, duration or need for repeat treatment.

Systematic review of microfocused ultrasound for facial tightening (2023).

Useful follow-up questions

  • Which outcome will be assessed: firmness, contour, volume or something else?
  • Which method will be used—scale, standardized photography, 3D imaging or patient report?
  • Why was this reassessment interval chosen?
  • How will a partial or unexpected response be discussed?
  • When would it make sense to revisit the plan, consider another option or not repeat treatment?

Ask the practitioner to explain which evidence matches the device and goal under discussion. Studies of other devices or areas do not establish the local service’s timeline; the practitioner should confirm that protocol.
