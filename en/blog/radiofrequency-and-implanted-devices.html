Radiofrequency and implanted devices: what should be confirmed?

Safety depends on the specific radiofrequency device and implant. Learn what information to gather and why there is no universal answer.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; confirm with the practitioner

01 · “Radiofrequency” does not identify one device

The procedure name alone cannot establish compatibility

Aesthetic radiofrequency equipment varies by model, applicator, configuration and intended use. Implants also differ: a pacemaker, defibrillator, neurostimulator, cochlear implant and metallic implant are not one interchangeable category.

It is therefore not accurate to conclude online that all radiofrequency is safe or prohibited for everyone with an implant. The answer depends on the exact model’s warnings, implant location and planned treatment area. The local service does not publicly identify the device manufacturer and model; confirm those details before deciding.

02 · Electronic implants need specific attention

Electromagnetic interference is a question for the responsible care teams

Cardiac-device manufacturers advise patients to tell healthcare teams about their implant and consult their physician about certain procedures. This general guidance supports an individual review, but it does not by itself answer whether a specific aesthetic device may be used.

Do not conflate different procedures: medical radiofrequency ablation, electrosurgery and aesthetic radiofrequency are not automatically equivalent. Each device’s labeling defines its own use context and warnings.

03 · The device manual can change the answer

Warnings for one product do not apply to every other product

As an example of why the model matters, Cutera publishes product-specific contraindications for Secret RF, including people with a cardiac pacemaker or another active implanted metal device in the treatment area. This describes that product; it does not establish that this clinic uses Secret RF.

Do not use one manufacturer’s page to infer the safety of another device. The care team should check the current instructions for use, applicator and exact intended use, alongside guidance from the implant manufacturer and the clinician responsible for the implant.

04 · What to bring and ask at the consultation

A practical check before booking or starting

Tell the practitioner the implant type and model, when and where it was placed, and whether you have received prior electromagnetic-interference guidance. If available, bring the device card or procedure record. Do not share identifiable medical details on this page.

Ask for the exact radiofrequency device model, the proposed treatment location, the warnings in its manual and whether the team needs input from the manufacturer or the clinician who follows the implant. The practitioner should confirm whether the available documentation is enough to decide; if the answer is unclear, the session should wait for that review.

Do not stop medication, change an implant’s programming or rely on someone else’s experience. Discuss these decisions with the professionals responsible for your care.
