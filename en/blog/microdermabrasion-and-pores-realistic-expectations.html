Microdermabrasion and pores: what can change in their appearance?

Learn why pores may look more visible, what exfoliation may change, and why microdermabrasion cannot permanently close pores.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Why can pores look more visible?

Pores are natural openings of hair follicles. Oiliness, clogging, inflammation around acne, and loss of skin firmness can make them more noticeable. Their appearance varies by person and facial area, so “large pores” alone does not identify a cause or determine a procedure.

What microdermabrasion can — and cannot — promise

The American Academy of Dermatology says exfoliation may make pores less noticeable in some situations when performed safely. This does not show that microdermabrasion closes pores, permanently changes their size, or suits every skin type. The AAD overview describes visible changes after a series of treatments; those findings do not guarantee an individual response or establish the device or protocol used at this clinic.

Oiliness, acne, texture and firmness raise different questions

Rough texture, blackheads, inflamed acne, flat marks and deep scars are not the same concern. Surface exfoliation does not replace assessment and treatment of acne, diagnose a lesion, or remodel deep scars. If there is active irritation, a changing spot or uncertainty about the cause, discuss it before choosing a resurfacing method.

What to bring to an assessment

Share any sensitivity, current acne, products and medicines you use, recent procedures, and a tendency to develop dark marks or scars. Ask which visible concern the procedure aims to address, what its limits are, and which signs warrant follow-up. Do not increase intensity or combine at-home exfoliants to chase faster results.
