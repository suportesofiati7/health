Botulinum toxin and migraine: are aesthetic use and prevention the same?

The same medicine can have different uses, but an aesthetic treatment is not the same as a clinical migraine-prevention plan. Product, indication, and assessment must be confirmed.

The same medicine family does not make the indications interchangeable

Botulinum toxin appears in both aesthetic and therapeutic settings. That does not mean every forehead treatment prevents migraine, or that a cosmetic plan can be used to treat a headache. Purpose, diagnosis, product, assessment, and protocol need to be identified separately.

The Brazilian professional leaflet for BOTOX® lists, for that specific product, treatment of hyperkinetic facial lines and prevention of chronic migraine in adults with refractory disease and significant impact on quality of life and daily activities. Another brand’s leaflet may list different indications. This does not confirm which product is used in the local aesthetic service.

What the clinical migraine evidence measured

The PREEMPT studies evaluated adults with chronic migraine and measured outcomes related to headache days, impact, and quality of life. A European Headache Federation guideline discusses onabotulinumtoxinA within the clinical context of chronic migraine. This evidence did not study aesthetic treatment of expression lines as migraine therapy, and it does not establish a pain benefit for someone seeking a facial result.

Likewise, aesthetic studies of expression lines measure specific cosmetic outcomes. They do not replace a neurological diagnosis or show that headaches will be prevented. Recurrent or disabling headache symptoms should be assessed by a qualified medical professional.

The product and treatment history matter

Botulinum toxin products should not be treated as interchangeable: indications, labeling, presentation, and safety information are product-specific. Anvisa advises patients to share previous injections, including date, indication, and prior dose, and to check the product’s brand, lot, and expiration date. If aesthetic and therapeutic treatments occur in different settings, tell each professional about both; do not combine or adjust treatments on your own.

Useful questions before an aesthetic appointment

  • Is this consultation about expression lines or an assessment of headache symptoms?
  • Which brand and indication are planned? Does that product’s current Brazilian leaflet include that use?
  • What history of migraine, other headaches, medicines, and previous injections should be shared?
  • Who will assess the headache, and what follow-up applies if the concern is therapeutic?
  • Which limits and risks belong to this particular product and indication?

The clinic should confirm the product actually used and its current leaflet before making a specific statement. The upper-face service described on this site is an aesthetic assessment; it is not an offer of medical migraine treatment.

References and limits

  • Brazilian BOTOX® professional leaflet — product-specific source for indications, warnings, and clinical evidence described by the manufacturer.
  • Aurora et al. PREEMPT 1: randomized trial of onabotulinumtoxinA in chronic migraine (2010) — evaluates headache outcomes in a defined clinical population.
  • European Headache Federation consensus on onabotulinumtoxinA in chronic migraine (2018) — recommendations for neurological treatment, not aesthetic use.
  • Anvisa warning about botulinum toxin (2025) — advises sharing previous treatment history and checking product details.

The local indication, current product leaflet, and professional scope must be confirmed by the responsible team. This educational page does not diagnose migraine or recommend starting, stopping, or combining treatments.
