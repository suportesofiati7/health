Cosmetic radiofrequency during pregnancy or breastfeeding: what is known?

Pregnancy and breastfeeding are not the same situation. Evidence specific to cosmetic radiofrequency is scarce; a recent review recommends postponing elective use during pregnancy and considering any procedure during breastfeeding cautiously and individually.

What the evidence can tell us

A 2026 narrative review of cosmetic dermatology during pregnancy and breastfeeding reports a lack of studies specific to cosmetic radiofrequency during pregnancy. The authors describe theoretical concerns about heating and energy deposition in deeper tissues, and recommend postponing elective radiofrequency device use during pregnancy. This is a cautious recommendation in the face of missing data; it does not show that an accidental exposure caused harm.

The review distinguishes breastfeeding from pregnancy: radiofrequency procedures may be considered cautiously, but that recommendation rests on limited evidence and individual assessment, not robust infant-outcome trials. The review is neither systematic nor an official protocol for every device. A 2022 review that discusses radiofrequency in medical dermatology also does not establish safety for an elective cosmetic treatment: indication, area, device and expected benefit differ.

Kovacs, International Journal of Dermatology (2026): narrative review of cosmetic dermatology in pregnancy and lactation · Dermatologic and Cosmetic Procedures in Pregnancy (2022).

Why the answer does not apply to every device

Radiofrequency is a family of devices. The name alone does not tell you whether energy is delivered through surface electrodes or needles, the treatment area, depth, intended use, manufacturer instructions or contraindications for that model. Literature on one system does not confirm which device is used at a local appointment.

The 2026 review groups cosmetic radiofrequency with other energy-based devices and acknowledges that recommendations rely mainly on reviews, indirect data, mechanism and expert opinion. It does not present evidence proving fetal harm from a particular model, nor does it establish that every device has the same exposure.

What to do before an appointment

Tell the practitioner if you are pregnant, might be pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding; also mention the proposed treatment area and any recent procedure. For elective cosmetic treatment, published recommendations favor postponing during pregnancy and revisiting the decision after delivery. If you are breastfeeding, ask the practitioner to confirm the device model and intended use, area, manufacturer instructions and evidence for considering that procedure. Do not assume advice for a different type of radiofrequency applies to the proposed device.

During breastfeeding, the review recommends avoiding procedures involving the breast or nipple. Also ask how to prevent direct infant contact with treated skin and what specific aftercare would be needed. The decision should be discussed with the responsible practitioner and, if there are clinical questions about pregnancy or breastfeeding, your obstetric care team.

Questions to bring to your appointment

  • Which model and applicator will be used, and what is the manufacturer’s intended use?
  • Is the proposed procedure elective or is there a separate medical need?
  • What evidence applies to this device and treatment area during breastfeeding?
  • How will treatment of the breast and infant contact with treated skin be avoided?
  • If I prefer to wait, when would it make sense to revisit the decision?
