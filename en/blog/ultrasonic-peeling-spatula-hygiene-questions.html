Ultrasonic peeling spatula hygiene: what should you ask?

A practical guide to reusable equipment in aesthetic services and questions that clarify how an ultrasonic spatula is handled.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; local protocol is unconfirmed

01 · Why ask about hygiene?

Reusable equipment needs an appropriate process

The ultrasonic-peeling service page describes a spatula used on skin. Understanding how reusable items are prepared can help you discuss safety; a surface that looks clean does not explain the process on its own.

Anvisa’s public guidance says reusable equipment, materials and utensils should undergo cleaning, disinfection and sterilisation after each use as necessary. The applicable steps depend on the item, its intended use, manufacturer instructions and local sanitary requirements.

02 · What general guidance cannot determine

Processing depends on classification and instructions

This general guidance cannot determine the method for a particular spatula model. It also does not confirm which device the clinic uses, whether a tip is disposable or reusable, or how the clinic processes it.

There is no reliable answer based only on “wiping it with alcohol” or looking at the surface. The team should follow the manufacturer’s instructions and the sanitary rules that apply to the item and its use.

03 · Straightforward questions before an appointment

Ask the team to explain its preparation process

  • Is the part that touches the skin disposable or reusable?
  • If reusable, how is it processed between appointments under the manufacturer’s instructions and applicable sanitary rules?
  • Are there parts that cannot be immersed or exposed to a particular method?
  • How does the team know the item is ready for use?
  • Would the appointment be postponed if assessment finds a lesion, irritation or another relevant skin condition?

You can ask for a clear explanation without trying to prescribe the technical method yourself. If the answer does not clarify the process, discuss it with the responsible practitioner before deciding.

04 · Hygiene and suitability are separate questions

A safe process does not establish that a procedure is right for you

Hygiene is one part of care. Assessment still needs to consider the concern, skin condition, goal and device intended use. The term “ultrasonic peeling” does not establish efficacy for acne, pigmentation or scars, and the service does not replace medical assessment when needed.
