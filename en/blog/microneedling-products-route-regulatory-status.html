Microneedling and products: when does the route change regulatory status?

Not every microneedling session includes a product application. If one is proposed, learn why the label, intended route and regulatory status need to match the use.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Clarify what is being proposed

Microneedling can be discussed without assuming a product is applied

The procedure name describes a needle-based technique, but it does not by itself say whether a product will be applied, at which step or for what purpose. The clinic’s public service page does not identify a product used with the procedure. Ask whether the proposal is microneedling without substance delivery or includes a product; those are different details for assessment.

02 · Route and category are not marketing details

External use does not mean use through punctures

Anvisa explains that cosmetics are intended for external, non-invasive use. When a substance is applied by injection or microneedling to enter the skin directly or reach deeper layers, it must be regularized in the appropriate category of medicine or health product, not as a cosmetic. A sterile label, packaging that accepts a needle or an aesthetic claim does not replace the authorized purpose and directions for use.

03 · What to check before deciding

Ask for product identity and instructions for use

If a product is proposed, ask for its name and manufacturer, regulatory category, regularization number, and what the label and instructions authorize. Confirm that the proposed route and purpose match that product’s documentation. Checking regularization helps verify status, but finding a listing does not prove that every method of application is authorized.

04 · Questions for a clear discussion

Clarify the technique and team’s role too

Ask what goal is attributed to microneedling alone and what goal, if any, is attributed to the product; who assesses indication and route compatibility; which risks, alternatives and aftercare were considered; and how the clinic identifies the product used. Anvisa regulates products but does not determine each profession’s scope by itself. These points depend on current documentation, applicable professional rules and confirmation from the team.

05 · References and limits

Sources consulted

Anvisa’s sources describe the general regulatory distinction; they do not identify the product, route or protocol used by the clinic. This article does not claim that the service includes a substance application and does not replace professional review of a product and its intended use.
