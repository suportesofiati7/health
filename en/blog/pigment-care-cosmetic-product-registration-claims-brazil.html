Skin-lightening products for melasma: what does an Anvisa search confirm?

A database search can confirm a product’s regulatory status, but it cannot diagnose a mark or decide whether a product is right for you. Learn what to check and what still requires assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Evidence-informed reading

This guide explains how to check public product information. It does not identify products used in the clinic’s protocol, recommend ingredients or replace diagnosis and professional guidance.

01 · Start with the right question

Regulatory status, intended use and individual indication are different things

Before choosing a product for a change in skin colour, it helps to know what is being assessed and what the goal of care is. The word “brightening” or a marketing promise does not by itself identify the regulatory category, authorized purpose or cause of pigmentation. An Anvisa search can help confirm the regulatory status shown for a product; it cannot determine whether a mark is melasma, post-inflammatory pigmentation or another condition.

02 · What an Anvisa search can confirm

Cosmetics may be registered or exempt from registration

Anvisa explains that some cosmetic groups require registration, while other products are exempt and go through notification. Therefore, not finding a registration number is not enough to conclude that a cosmetic is irregular: the product must be searched under the correct category and query type. The official search can use the product or brand name, company/CNPJ, and process or registration details shown on the label.

03 · Read the result carefully

“Active” or “valid” describes the regulatory status being checked

On its regularized-products page, Anvisa says an active or valid result means the product is regularized. Check that the search matches the exact name, company, presentation and category shown during the consultation. This status does not confirm a diagnosis, prove that the product is suitable for every person with pigmentation, or guarantee an individual clinical result.

04 · A cosmetic has its own purpose and route

External use is not the same as introducing a substance into the skin

Anvisa defines cosmetics as products for external use. Products intended to enter the skin through injection or microneedling should not be treated as cosmetics; they need the appropriate sanitary category. A cosmetic-search result does not confirm authorization for another route, purpose or procedure. If a product is associated with microchannels, injection or in-office application, ask for the exact name, category and intended route.

05 · A label claim is not an assessment of a skin lesion

Pigmentation can have different causes

Melasma, post-inflammatory marks, sun-related changes and other lesions are not interchangeable. Anvisa says cosmetics must not have a therapeutic purpose; claims that suggest treatment of a disease call for careful attention to product category and official information. Even a regularized product does not replace assessment of a new, changing or symptomatic mark, or one whose cause is unclear.

06 · Questions that help in a consultation

Ask for product details and purpose before comparing promises

  • What is the full product name, manufacturer and presentation?
  • Which Anvisa category should I search, and which label detail identifies the process?
  • Is the proposed use external or does it involve another route?
  • Which condition is being assessed, and what evidence supports this purpose?
  • Which alternatives and general care belong in the discussion, without assuming an individual protocol?

These questions help verify information; they do not let someone diagnose their own skin or choose treatment from a label alone.
