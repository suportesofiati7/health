Hair loss: when might testing be part of an evaluation?

Learn how medical evaluation of hair loss may begin and why blood tests or a biopsy are considered only when the clinical assessment calls for them.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Evaluation starts with a history and scalp examination

Medical evaluation often begins with questions about when the change started, whether it was sudden or gradual, which areas are affected, and what was happening with your health. A dermatologist also examines the scalp and hair; distribution, skin findings and breakage can help shape possible explanations. An appointment does not automatically begin with a fixed list of tests.

When tests may be considered

The American Academy of Dermatology explains that if the suspected cause involves disease, nutritional deficiency, hormonal imbalance or infection, a dermatologist may consider blood tests or a scalp biopsy. Not everyone needs these tests, and an isolated result should not be interpreted outside its context. The choice depends on what the history and examination suggest.

Why there is no universal hair-loss test panel

Diffuse shedding, localized patches, gradual thinning and breakage can have different causes — and more than one can coexist. Copying another person’s tests, ordering a broad panel on your own or starting supplements without guidance does not replace an assessment. Bring results you already have; do not stop prescribed medicine or start supplements because of this article.

How to prepare for a dermatology discussion

Write down when you noticed the change, how it progressed, scalp symptoms, recent illnesses, surgery, major weight changes, medicines and family history. Bring prior results and, if available, comparable photos over time. Ask what question each proposed test is meant to answer and who will follow up on the results.

How this relates to aesthetic scalp procedures

Scalp mesotherapy and MMP do not identify the cause of hair loss and do not replace medical diagnosis or treatment. Dermatologic evaluation may be appropriate before discussing an aesthetic procedure, especially when the change is recent, rapid, localized or accompanied by symptoms. Any decision depends on the individual situation and verification of the offered protocol.
