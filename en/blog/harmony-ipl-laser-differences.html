IPL and laser: how do they differ on a light platform?

One device may offer different sources and handpieces. So “Harmony,” “laser,” or “light” alone does not identify what will be used or prove that two proposals are equivalent.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; confirm the device and indication

IPL and laser describe different sources

Laser refers to light emission with specific wavelength and beam characteristics. IPL (intense pulsed light) uses pulses across a broader range of wavelengths, selected through filters and the applicator. Both use light-based technology, but they are different modalities and should not be compared only by name or by how a session feels.

Response and risk depend on the target, area, skin characteristics, equipment and how the technology is applied. A marketing label does not replace identification of the device, handpiece and intended purpose.

A platform may combine several handpieces

Manufacturer Alma describes the Harmony XL PRO family as a multi-application platform that may include different technologies and handpieces, including AFT/IPL applicators and different lasers. The manufacturer notes that the configuration is modular. This explains the product category; it does not verify the version installed, its configuration, Brazilian regularisation or the handpiece available at this clinic.

This distinction matters when someone compares treatments for pigmentation, redness, texture or hair: each goal may involve a different source and assessment.

What to confirm before comparing proposals

  • What are the exact manufacturer, model and version?
  • Which source and handpiece will be used: IPL/AFT or a specific laser?
  • What is the goal of the proposal and which target is being considered?
  • How do skin characteristics, recent sun exposure, medicines and recent procedures factor into the assessment?
  • What intended use appears in the manual, and what is the device’s status in Brazilian regulatory sources?
  • Which alternatives, risks, care instructions and limits on outcomes have been explained?

These answers support a concrete comparison. They do not allow patients to choose settings or determine their own indication.

What evidence from one modality cannot prove

A study of a specific laser, filter, handpiece or indication does not automatically show that another device produces the same effect. Even when the platform brand matches, the configuration and protocol in a study may differ. Check which device was studied, in which population and for what goal before applying a finding to a different treatment discussion.

The public service page mentions a Harmony platform but does not identify the model and handpieces available for care. The practitioner should confirm those details before making a specific claim about indication or outcome.
