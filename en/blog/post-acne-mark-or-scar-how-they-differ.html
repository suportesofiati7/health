Post-acne mark or scar: differences that change assessment

A flat mark after acne is not necessarily a scar. Learn why color, elevation and texture lead to different questions before considering treatment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · A flat mark may be post-inflammatory pigmentation

Not every mark left after acne is a scar

When an acne lesion clears, a flat darker or red area may remain. The American Academy of Dermatology describes these marks as post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH), which is different from a structural scar. Color varies with skin tone and may change over time; a photograph alone cannot confirm the cause.

02 · Depressions and raised areas suggest a different change

Color and texture answer different questions

Acne scars can create depressions or raised areas. Elevation, depressions, itching, pain and texture change are different details from the color of a flat mark. More than one type may be present, and active acne can continue to contribute to new marks. Assessment should consider history and the skin in person rather than relying on the name someone uses for the concern.

03 · Pigmentation and scarring do not automatically share one plan

The cause changes the treatment conversation

An approach aimed at pigment is not automatically suitable for a depressed or raised scar; procedures aimed at texture are not synonymous with treating a mark. US dermatology guidance describes different options according to scar type and says flat post-inflammatory marks are not scars. This can inform questions but does not determine an individual indication or a protocol in Brazil.

04 · Describe how the mark appeared and changed

Prepare for assessment without self-diagnosing

Describe when the mark appeared, whether acne or irritation came first, whether the surface is flat or uneven and how it has changed. Ask whether the main concern is color, elevation or both, which professional should assess it and which options correspond to each goal.
