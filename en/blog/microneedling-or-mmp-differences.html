Microneedling or MMP: what differs and what to confirm

Microneedling and MMP can both involve microchannels, but their names alone do not clarify the device, substance delivery or protocol. Learn what to compare.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Microchannels can serve different purposes

The methods overlap

Microneedling is a broad term for techniques that create small channels in the skin. In some contexts, the procedure aims to produce a local skin response; in others, microneedling devices are studied as ways to facilitate transdermal substance delivery. This creates an area of overlap with microinfusion. “Microneedling” and “MMP” should therefore be neither treated as exact synonyms nor assumed to be unrelated methods.

02 · MMP is described as a microinfusion technique

What MMP describes in the literature

In the literature, MMP—microinfusion of medications into the skin—is described as a delivery technique that may use tattoo devices to create microchannels and deposit substances. That published description does not prove that a specific clinic uses the same device, product, depth, purpose or protocol. It also does not turn every cosmetic into a medicine or confirm that a particular substance is appropriate for delivery through the skin.

03 · Mechanism and indication are different kinds of evidence

Delivery evidence does not prove clinical benefit

A 2026 narrative review summarises MMP studies across varied indications, but it does not establish efficacy for every product, condition or practitioner. A meta-analysis of microneedling-assisted drug delivery included delivery studies, including laboratory diffusion experiments; showing that a substance crosses a barrier more readily does not by itself demonstrate clinical improvement or safety for a specific indication. Results depend on the product, device, application and context.

04 · Confirm the device, product and scope

Questions about the service being offered

Before comparing services, ask which technique and device will be used; whether a product will be delivered and its name, regulatory category, route and indication; how evidence applies to that use; who is authorised to perform the technique; and which risks, alternatives and aftercare will be explained. These details need confirmation from the practitioner and applicable documentation. Read about the listed services: microneedling and MMP. They do not establish individual suitability.
