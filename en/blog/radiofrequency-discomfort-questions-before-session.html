Radiofrequency and discomfort: questions before a session

The word “radiofrequency” alone does not predict how a session will feel. Learn how to ask about the device, treatment area and communication with the team.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Radiofrequency includes different devices and techniques

Modality, applicator, area, settings, contact time and individual characteristics can change the experience. Radiofrequency applied over the skin and microneedling radiofrequency are also different interventions. Because the model and protocol available at the clinic are not identified here, it is not possible to promise a specific sensation, intensity or recovery time.

Comfort studies describe the devices they evaluated

A patient-experience study of monopolar radiofrequency enrolled 50 women, but 34 completed the follow-up questionnaire; the authors described mild-to-moderate discomfort in that context. A 2026 prospective study evaluated a 40.68-MHz unipolar modality in 30 participants, of whom 26 completed follow-up. These studies used their own devices, protocols and assessment methods. Their results cannot predict a session with a different device.

Agree on how to alert the team

Before starting, ask what sensation may occur with the proposed model and area and how the team would like you to signal heat or discomfort. Agree on a signal to pause and talk. If a sensation becomes strong, painful or unexpected, tell the practitioner right away; you do not need to endure it silently for a treatment to “work.”

Useful questions before deciding

  • What is the exact manufacturer and model of the device?
  • Will the application be external or involve microneedles? Which applicator will be used?
  • What do studies of this model report about sensation, and what are their limits?
  • How can I request a pause or communicate discomfort during the session?
  • Which signs afterwards require contacting the team?
  • Is there any health or previous-treatment history I should share beforehand?

Ask for aftercare and contact instructions in writing. Do not assume that devices with the same commercial name, or studies using the same energy category, create the same experience or carry the same warnings.

What the evidence cannot tell you about this clinic

The studies cited describe experiences measured with specific devices; they do not identify the model used here or establish an average pain level for every person.
