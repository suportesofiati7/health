Asymptomatic spider veins: is routine duplex ultrasound needed?

A 2023 vascular-society consensus advises against routine duplex ultrasound for asymptomatic telangiectasias or reticular veins. Learn what that guidance does and does not establish.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

Appearance alone does not define a venous condition

Small reddish or bluish vessels may be noticeable even without pain or swelling. Clinical class C1 includes telangiectasias and reticular veins, but only a clinician can assess whether it describes an individual case. Do not classify yourself from a photograph or symptom checklist.

Duplex is not recommended routinely for every asymptomatic case

A consensus from US vascular societies says lower-extremity venous duplex ultrasound should not be performed routinely in asymptomatic people with C1 telangiectasias or reticular veins. It notes that testing could lead to interventions on larger veins that may not be needed. This is a context-specific recommendation: it does not conclude that scans are never useful and does not establish a Brazilian rule for every aesthetic procedure.

Individual assessment still matters

The same consensus says a clinician may consider duplex in specific situations, such as bleeding or severe pain/burning associated with moderate or extensive vessels. This is not a self-diagnosis checklist or an automatic test order. Symptoms, findings, history and local professional rules need to be considered by the person assessing the patient.

Understand what decision the result could inform

Ask which clinical question is being investigated, why the scan was considered and what might change depending on the result. Confirm who orders and interprets duplex and which professional should follow the concern. The guide on what duplex does explains other assessment contexts; the PEIM page describes the service without replacing vascular assessment.

Source and limits

The source below is a consensus from US vascular societies. Its recommendation addresses a specific clinical context and does not replace Brazilian guidance, individual assessment or confirmation of local protocols and professional scope.

  • SVS / AVF / AVLS — 2023 clinical practice guidelines, Part II (consensus statements 1.5.1–1.5.2)
