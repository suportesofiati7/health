Ultraformer MPT: numbness or facial weakness after treatment

Tingling, numbness and a movement change are not the same. Note when they began and seek appropriate guidance; do not assume the cause or wait for a generic timeline.

If you notice a change

Describe what changed and when

  1. Area and time it began.
  2. Sensation or movement affected.
  3. Whether it is improving, stable or worsening.

Describe pain, tingling and weakness clearly

Studies of some microfocused ultrasound devices report local tenderness or tingling; reports also describe rare events involving sensory or motor function. This does not make it possible to determine online whether a symptom after a session was caused by the device, expected for that person or how long it should last.

The manufacturer’s Ultraformer MPT instructions for use describe transient local muscle weakness and transient numbness that may occur due to inflammation of motor or sensory nerves. The document does not estimate individual frequency or replace clinical assessment. The local service page does not publish which transducers, settings or protocol are used for each area; confirm which manual version applies to the clinic’s device.

What to do after noticing a symptom

Contact the responsible team promptly if numbness, persistent tingling, increasing pain or difficulty moving part of the face appears after the procedure. Report when it began, the treated area, how it changed, any useful photos or videos, and any other changes. Do not massage, heat, medicate or have another procedure on the area without clinical guidance.

The team may advise an in-person assessment or referral. An online report or photograph cannot distinguish nerve irritation, a local change or another cause; a new session should wait until a professional has clarified the concern.

Recognize signs that should not wait for the clinic to reply

Sudden facial weakness or asymmetry can also be a stroke warning sign. If it occurs with speech changes, arm or leg weakness or numbness, confusion, vision changes, loss of balance or a sudden severe headache, contact emergency services immediately — even if the signs improve. Use your local emergency number; in Brazil, call 192 (SAMU) or go to an emergency department without driving yourself.

If isolated facial weakness began suddenly, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not assume it is a procedure effect; time-sensitive conditions must first be ruled out.

What the evidence can and cannot tell us

A safety review of microfocused ultrasound with visualization (MFU-V) described paraesthesia and paresis among rare events reported for Ultherapy, a specific technology. A 2026 complications review notes that the literature is heterogeneous and largely made up of case series and reports. These findings do not estimate frequency for Ultraformer MPT or confirm cause or outcome for a different device.

The Ultraformer MPT manual and studies of other systems are sources for a safety discussion, not substitutes for the manual applicable to the local model, clinical assessment or emergency guidance.

References and limits

  • Classys: Ultraformer MPT instructions for use in Singapore’s Ministry of Health regulatory repository — lists possible transient effects involving motor and sensory nerves; confirm the version and instructions applicable to the local device.
  • Hitchcock et al. (2014): MFU-V safety review — data for Ultherapy/MFU-V, not an estimate for Ultraformer MPT.
  • 2026 review and consensus on MFU-V complications — synthesizes events and limitations in a heterogeneous literature.
  • American Stroke Association: stroke warning signs — sudden signs require immediate contact with emergency services; use the local number.
