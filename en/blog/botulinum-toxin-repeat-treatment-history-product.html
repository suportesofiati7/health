Repeating botulinum toxin: what product and treatment history to share?

Before repeating botulinum toxin, organize the dates, indication and product from prior injections. Learn what Brazil’s Anvisa recommends and why intervals depend on product labeling.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Why prior treatment history matters

Before another treatment, share whether you have received botulinum toxin, when, for what indication and which product was used, if known. Brazil’s Anvisa advises clinicians to ask about prior injections, including date, indication and previous dose, to help check appropriate intervals. Also record what you noticed afterward and any unwanted effects.

Brand, authorization and intervals are not interchangeable details

Botulinum toxin products have their own prescribing information. Anvisa advises following the product label and says the interval may vary by product; the health professional should guide the patient. Units and schedules for one product should not be converted or copied to another on your own. This page does not determine when anyone should repeat treatment and does not confirm which product the clinic uses.

What to bring or ask at consultation

If available, bring your previous treatment record. Ask for the medicine name, date and treated area; confirm the brand, lot and expiry date of the new product and ask which label and interval apply to your case. Also mention other recent treatments and health changes. If you do not know a detail, say so rather than guessing the dose or product.

Serious symptoms should not wait for a routine follow-up

Difficulty swallowing, speaking or breathing, weakness beyond the treated area or rapid worsening need immediate medical care. Anvisa warns that botulism symptoms after treatment may begin hours or weeks later. For a non-urgent question, use the contact route provided by the care team.
