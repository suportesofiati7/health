Ultraformer MPT and a 3D study: what do millimeter measurements mean?

A study of 50 patients measured facial changes immediately after Ultraformer MPT. Learn what a millimeter measurement says and what it cannot establish.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading based on published research

01 · What the 3D study measured

A distance in millimeters describes a measurement, not a promise

A 2024 paper analysed three-dimensional images taken immediately before and after HIFU with Ultraformer MPT across seven facial areas. Its table reports mean differences in millimeters. That number answers a geometric question within the study design; it is not a direct measure of satisfaction and does not, by itself, say how long a change lasts.

The study selected 50 participants with suitable images from 128 records at one clinic. The analysis was retrospective and included women aged 34 to 81. Those details matter when considering who the findings may apply to.

02 · What the design cannot answer

An immediate before-and-after comparison does not show duration

Post-treatment images were taken immediately. The study therefore did not track whether a change persisted for months, compare the device with another option or predict one person’s response. A measured change in one area also does not establish how every participant perceived the result.

The authors note limitations including a small sample, a single centre and some subjectivity in measurement. A statistically detectable group difference is not automatically a meaningful difference for each patient.

03 · How to read the published numbers

Area, method and photo timing are part of the result

The paper assessed seven areas and used a protocol adapted to anatomy. Mean distances should not be turned into a ranking of the “best areas” for everyone: this was not a comparative trial between regions or treatments, and participant and image selection limit generalisation.

A contour measurement alone also cannot answer whether someone’s goal is firmness, preserving volume, changing fat or something else. Those questions need to be discussed separately. Read about facial volume, area and goals and discomfort and recovery.

04 · Questions before applying the study to your situation

Ask for context about both the evidence and the proposal

  • What change is being considered, and in which area?
  • Did the cited evidence examine the same device, mode, area and follow-up timing?
  • When were images or measurements taken, and how were they standardised?
  • What did the study not follow, such as duration or comparisons with alternatives?
  • What is realistic for my goal, given the remaining uncertainty?

These questions can support a discussion about the Ultraformer MPT service without turning a study result into an individual prediction.

05 · Source and limits

Primary source

The paper is a single-centre analysis of selected participants with immediate assessment. Its conclusion does not confirm the local configuration, protocol or outcome, and it does not replace professional review of individual suitability.
