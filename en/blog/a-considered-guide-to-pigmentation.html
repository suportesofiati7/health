Melasma and dark spots: causes, sun protection and care

Melasma and dark spots: assessment, sun protection and realistic options can sound simple in a search. In practice, a responsible decision depends on history, current skin or scalp condition, routine and the goal behind the question.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Start with the context

Start with the context

A “dark spot” describes an appearance, not a cause. Melasma, marks left after inflammation and other pigment changes can look similar in photographs but call for different questions. Note when it began, where it appears, whether it changes with sun or heat, whether acne or irritation came first, and what products or medicines are part of your routine. A new or persistent change that concerns you deserves professional assessment; you do not need to arrive with a diagnosis.

02 · The technique comes after the question

The technique comes after the question

For melasma, the American Academy of Dermatology highlights protection from sunlight and, for some people, visible light. Shade, physical barriers and sunscreen that fits your routine can help limit triggers; a tinted option containing iron oxides can be discussed with a professional. The best plan is one you can use consistently: texture, shade, reapplication and your actual exposure all matter alongside the label.

03 · What is useful to observe

What is useful to observe

There is no universally best procedure for melasma. AAD guidance describes sun protection and topical treatment as common parts of dermatologic care; procedures may be considered in selected situations, but evidence and suitability vary. Irritation and inflammation can also leave marks in some skin. Ask what may be causing the patch, which alternatives exist, what benefit is realistic, what risks of darkening or recurrence should be discussed, and who will monitor changes. Gradual control is a more honest expectation than a promise to erase it.
