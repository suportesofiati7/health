PEIM: what should be agreed about aftercare and follow-up?

Preparation, aftercare and follow-up depend on the procedure and product used. Learn what to ask the care team before treating small vessels.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; confirm the local protocol

01 · Ask for the plan for this specific appointment

“PEIM” alone does not explain aftercare

The service page describes an injectable approach for selected superficial vessels, but does not publicly identify the product, complete technique or post-procedure instructions. Without those details, it is not appropriate to publish a universal recovery timeline, recommend compression or promise how many sessions will be needed.

Before deciding, ask the practitioner to explain the planned steps and provide written instructions. Confirm which product will be used, which area will be treated, what may happen in the first few days and how to get help if you have a question or notice a reaction.

02 · Aftercare varies with the technique

Do not transfer advice from a different procedure

Brazilian guidelines for chronic venous disease discuss compression after some small-vessel treatments, including sclerotherapy, laser and radiofrequency. This illustrates that aftercare is method-specific; it does not prove that every approach called PEIM is the same technique or establish an individual prescription.

Ask whether compression, a temporary restriction or local care applies to your case, for how long and why. Follow only the instructions given by the team responsible for the specific procedure and product; do not improvise stockings, bandages, medication or activity changes.

03 · Plan follow-up and outcome assessment

Appearance right after a session may not answer every question

Agree when and with whom follow-up will happen, how the team will assess progress and what to do if some vessels remain visible or a reaction appears. The service page does not publish a standard interval between sessions or return schedule, so these details need to be set during assessment.

Note the procedure date, treated area, product identified and instructions received. If the team recommends photographs, ask how to record the area consistently and share images privately. A photograph does not replace an examination when the care team considers an in-person assessment necessary.

04 · Questions to bring before starting

Know whom to contact and what changes to watch for

Ask the team to clarify: which aftercare applies to this product and technique? Should I use compression or change an activity? What changes may occur and for how long? When should I return? Which signs should prompt contact with the clinic or medical assessment? How can I reach the team outside procedure hours?

Answers should come from the responsible practitioner, based on the method actually used and your history. If aftercare, follow-up and contact instructions are still unclear, resolve those questions before the session.

05 · References and limits

References and limits

Venous-disease guidelines and sclerotherapy guidance do not identify the product or method used at this clinic. They provide context; the practitioner must explain the local protocol.
