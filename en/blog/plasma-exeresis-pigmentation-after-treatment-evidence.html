Plasma exeresis and pigmentation after treatment: what do studies show?

Post-inflammatory pigmentation has been reported after some procedures called plasma exeresis. These reports identify a possibility, but they do not calculate an individual’s risk or apply to every device called “plasma.”

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational content; professional review recommended

What “pigmentation after plasma” means

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation is a change in skin color that can follow inflammation or skin injury. For plasma exeresis, the published evidence includes case reports and small observational studies. This documents that the change has occurred, but its frequency and individual risk factors are not well established.

What the studies actually observed

One report described two cases of periorbital hyperpigmentation after plasma exeresis. Another observational study of 45 people treated for benign facial lesions recorded transient pigmentation in three participants. These studies involved specific devices, areas, participants and protocols; they are not reliable risk estimates for other devices or indications.

A finding from cold atmospheric plasma must not be used to predict the response to a focal thermal discharge. The word “plasma” alone does not identify the physical modality.

Why this cannot predict one person’s outcome

Skin characteristics, treatment area, condition, delivered energy, sun exposure and aftercare may affect recovery, but the available studies cannot turn these factors into an individual prediction. They also do not show that one preventive routine works for everyone. Do not start lightening agents or other products on your own after a procedure.

Useful questions before considering the technology

  • What are the exact manufacturer, model and modality?
  • Did the cited study evaluate the same kind of device, treatment area and purpose?
  • What color changes or healing issues are possible, and how will follow-up work?
  • Which protection and contact instructions apply to the actual protocol?
  • Who should assess new, persistent or worsening discoloration?

References and limits

  • Report of periorbital hyperpigmentation after plasma exeresis (two cases)
  • Observational study of voltaic-arc dermabrasion for benign facial lesions (45 participants)
  • Descriptive study of plasma-exeresis applications and recovery

This is limited evidence tied to specific modalities and protocols. It does not establish a universal pigmentation rate or the performance of the clinic’s device.
