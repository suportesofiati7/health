Platysma toxin: did the trial assess muscle bands or the whole jawline?

A phase 3 trial assessed the appearance of platysma bands and prominence during contraction. Its findings do not, by themselves, answer questions about loose skin, submental fullness or every aspect of jawline contour.

What did “platysma prominence” mean in the trial?

The phase 3 randomized trial studied onabotulinumtoxinA in adults with moderate or severe platysma prominence. It assessed neck bands and jawline appearance at maximum contraction, using scales rated separately by investigator and participant. The research question was therefore tied to visible muscle activity, not every possible cause of a neck or lower-face contour concern.

Loose skin, submental fat, submandibular glands, a double-chin appearance and other features may contribute to contour concerns. A paper about platysma bands does not show that toxin treats those other causes or that everyone with a similar concern matches the population studied.

The result depends on how “response” was defined

On day 14, 32.3% of the treatment group and 1.9% of the placebo group met the primary composite endpoint in the intention-to-treat population: at least a two-grade improvement to a minimal or mild rating on both the investigator’s and participant’s assessments. Other reported percentages refer to only one of those assessments. They are not the share of everyone who noticed any change, nor a prediction for an individual.

Participants were followed for 120 days after one treatment. The trial does not tell us how long the effect lasts for each person, what happens with repeated treatment over the long term, or how other botulinum toxin products compare.

A positive trial still has limits and conflicts to consider

The trial reported similar adverse-event incidence in the two groups and no dysphagia or muscle weakness among its participants. That is a result in that sample and follow-up period; it does not prove an absence of risk. The paper also discloses financial relationships between several authors and the product manufacturer, including employment, consulting and research support. Consider this alongside the design and outcomes when interpreting the findings.

The evidence applies to a specific onabotulinumtoxinA formulation, selected adults with moderate-to-severe prominence, and assessment during contraction. It does not automatically validate another brand, another indication, use in the local service, or practice outside applicable Brazilian rules.

Questions to bring to an assessment

  • Is my concern mainly a band visible on contraction, loose skin at rest, fullness, or a combination?
  • What did the practitioner observe at rest and during movement?
  • Which product and indication are being considered, and what evidence matches them?
  • Which limits, possible adverse effects and alternatives matter for my history?
  • How will the result be assessed, and when should I contact the team about an unexpected symptom?

These questions organize a discussion; they do not diagnose the cause or select a treatment. Individual assessment and confirmation of the local product, indication and protocol remain necessary.

References and limits

  • Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of platysma prominence — one treatment, 120-day follow-up and contraction-based scale outcomes; authors disclosed financial relationships with the manufacturer.
  • U.S. BOTOX Cosmetic label — a U.S. regulatory reference for that product and its approved studies; it does not replace Brazilian labeling or confirmation of the product used locally.
