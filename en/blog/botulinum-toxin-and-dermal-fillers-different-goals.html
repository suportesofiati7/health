Botulinum toxin and dermal fillers: different goals

Botulinum toxin and dermal fillers are not interchangeable. Learn the general difference between muscle movement and volume, and what to ask about each option.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Different mechanisms and goals

One approach affects movement; another may add volume

Botulinum toxin is a medicine that temporarily reduces muscle activity in the treated area. Dermal fillers are a different product category: some are used to add volume or smooth certain contours, depending on the material, area and authorized use. They are not different names for the same procedure, and approved products and rules vary by country.

02 · Expression lines and volume are different concerns

The concern helps frame the discussion

A line that appears with movement and a concern involving volume may lead to different discussions. An assessment needs to understand the goal, anatomy, expectations, history and limits of each option; a photo or label cannot decide this. Not every visible feature requires an injection, and other approaches or choosing not to treat may also be considered.

04 · What to clarify in an assessment

Questions for an informed decision

Ask whether the concern relates to movement or volume; which options are actually available; which benefits and risks should be explained; how the product and indication are verified; what alternatives exist; and who will follow up. Read the botulinum toxin service pages for the upper face and the platysma and lower face. This guide does not determine which procedure is suitable for an individual.
