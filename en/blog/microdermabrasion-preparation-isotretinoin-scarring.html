Microdermabrasion: what to share about isotretinoin and scarring

Medicines and scarring history can change the conversation before exfoliation. Learn what to share without stopping medication or applying universal timelines.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Share medicines and recent skin changes

The consultation starts with your history

Before microdermabrasion, share current and recent medicines, previous procedures, irritation or open areas, a tendency to develop dark marks, and how cuts or prior treatments healed. Do not stop a medicine to try to qualify for a procedure. The person assessing you needs to know the actual product and technique as well as your current skin condition.

02 · AAD advises discussing isotretinoin before microdermabrasion

Mention isotretinoin use

The American Academy of Dermatology advises people taking or who have taken isotretinoin—particularly within the previous six months, in its microdermabrasion guidance—to consult a dermatologist before having the procedure at a spa or using an at-home kit because complications may occur. That interval belongs to the cited US guidance; it should not become an automatic timeline for every product, device, technique or setting in Brazil.

03 · Mention if you tend to scar easily

Scarring tendency also matters

The AAD also advises discussing microdermabrasion with a dermatologist if you scar easily. This does not predict an individual response, and the name “microdermabrasion” does not identify a clinic’s device or intensity. The practitioner should review your history and explain when additional medical assessment is appropriate.

04 · Confirm the decision and timing with the responsible team

Do not turn general guidance into personal instructions

Bring the medicine name and dates used, describe prior reactions and ask whether postponement, adaptation or dermatology assessment is appropriate. Request specific written instructions; do not use a general article to stop treatment or choose a date. Read about crystal microdermabrasion and diamond peel, and see when sensitive skin may call for a timing discussion.
