Microdermabrasion or dermabrasion: what is the difference?

The names sound alike, but describe different procedures. Microdermabrasion is superficial exfoliation; dermabrasion is a deeper medical procedure with different recovery and purpose.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

Why the names are confusing

Both words describe skin abrasion, but “micro” is not a cosmetic detail. The American Academy of Dermatology and the American Society for Dermatologic Surgery describe microdermabrasion as superficial removal of the outermost layer with an abrasive device tip. Systems may use particles or a coated tip; the local setup needs confirmation.

Dermabrasion is deeper. The AAD describes it as a surgical procedure used by dermatologists to deeply exfoliate and treat selected concerns, including some scars. Assessment, anesthesia, performance and healing differ from superficial exfoliation.

How the difference changes expectations

Microdermabrasion may help the appearance of surface texture or uneven tone in selected situations, but it is not equivalent to a technique that can remodel deeper scars. Dermabrasion should not be treated as a “stronger” option that is automatically appropriate: it is a different intervention with its own risks, selection and recovery.

Evidence for one does not establish the outcome of the other. For a scar concern, it first matters whether the feature is raised, indented, pigmented or a flat mark. A microdermabrasion page cannot select an approach for an individual scar.

Questions to ask the practitioner

  • Which procedure is being proposed: microdermabrasion, dermabrasion or another resurfacing method?
  • Which device and tip will be used, and what depth or goal is intended?
  • Is the concern surface texture, a mark, a scar or another condition?
  • What risks, recovery, follow-up and alternatives apply to the actual option?
  • If dermabrasion is discussed, which qualified professional will assess and perform it?
