Can microdermabrasion treat acne scars? Understanding its limits

Depressed scars, flat marks and raised scars differ. Learn what dermatology guidance says about microdermabrasion and why assessment depends on the type of mark.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Flat marks, depressions and raised scars are different

“Acne scar” can describe different marks

After acne, a flat mark may remain even when no structural scar is present. Depressed scars create pits or unevenness; raised scars have a visible or palpable elevation. This distinction matters because “texture” alone does not identify the type of change or which approach might be discussed. Active acne is a separate consideration.

02 · A surface mechanism does not predict scar correction

Surface exfoliation is not the same as scar remodeling

Microdermabrasion is superficial mechanical exfoliation. The fact that a technique renews the surface does not mean it can lift a depression, release tethering or remodel a raised scar. Scar type and depth, skin tone, current acne and healing history shape the conversation; a photograph or the word “peel” cannot answer these questions by itself.

03 · AAD does not present microdermabrasion as a direct scar treatment

Dermatology guidance sets limits for microdermabrasion

The American Academy of Dermatology describes microdermabrasion as gentle exfoliation and says it does not directly treat acne scars, although it may even skin tone and support appearance when used with another scar treatment. Its guidance distinguishes options for depressed and raised scars. This is US dermatology guidance, not an individual recommendation or confirmation of a Brazilian clinic’s scope or protocol.

04 · Identify the goal before comparing techniques

Start with the right assessment question

Ask whether your concern is a flat mark, depressed scar or raised scar; what realistic goal is proposed; which evidence applies to the available technique; and whether dermatology assessment is appropriate. Compare the listed crystal microdermabrasion and diamond peel pages without assuming a handpiece change alters which scar type can be treated. For scar and texture distinctions, also read the acne-scar guide.
