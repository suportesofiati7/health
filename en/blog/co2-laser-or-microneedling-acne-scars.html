CO₂ laser or microneedling for acne scars: what should you compare?

A careful comparison of two distinct approaches to atrophic acne scars, with evidence limits and questions for an individual assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Evidence-informed reading

This guide compares evidence for atrophic acne scars; it is not a diagnosis or a recommendation for a specific procedure. It does not assume which device or protocol the clinic uses.

01 · First confirm that the concern is a scar

Flat marks and structural scars call for different discussions

A flat mark left after acne may be pigmentation rather than a change in skin contour. Depressed scars, raised scars and color changes are not the same, and “uneven texture” alone does not establish the cause. The American Academy of Dermatology advises considering scar type, skin tone, active acne and treatment history when building a plan. If inflammatory breakouts are still occurring, controlling acne is often an important part of the discussion before treating scars.

02 · Two procedures, different patterns of action

What the terms describe, without assuming the local protocol

Fractional CO₂ laser creates microscopic zones of thermal treatment, leaving areas of skin between them untreated; fractional mode and settings change intensity and recovery. Mechanical microneedling uses needles to create controlled micro-injuries. These are different mechanisms, and “microneedling” does not automatically mean radiofrequency, plasma, growth factors or other products. The exact laser model, mode and Franciele’s protocol need to be confirmed in consultation; the studies discussed here do not prove the clinic uses the same protocols.

03 · What comparisons can tell us

There is no universal answer to “which works better?”

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis included 17 studies of fractional CO₂ laser and needling procedures for facial atrophic scars. It found no statistically significant difference between methods in mean scar-score reduction; variation across studies was high, so this does not prove the treatments are equivalent. The review found more post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation with CO₂ than with conventional microneedling. It also grouped distinct comparisons—for example, radiofrequency microneedling—which should not be confused with mechanical microneedling as a separate service.

04 · Pigment risk needs context

Skin tone and personal history belong in the decision

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation can follow skin inflammation or injury. The comparative review’s finding is a reason to discuss pigmentation; it cannot predict an individual outcome or define an absolute risk for this clinic’s protocol. Share a history of marks after acne, procedures, irritation or sun exposure, recent color changes and treatments already used. The studied evidence does not replace assessment of the diagnosis, skin type, scar and exact device.

05 · Recovery and discomfort cannot be inferred from the name

Ask about the actual plan being considered

There is no single recovery timeline that applies to every CO₂ laser or microneedling procedure. Treatment area, depth, settings, technique, skin and individual response matter. Study protocols and outcome measures varied; the literature cannot transfer a session calendar or recovery promise to this clinic. Before deciding, ask which exact modality would be used, what signs are expected, how to get aftercare guidance and which changes should prompt contact or medical assessment.

06 · Some situations may change the timing

Active acne and prior treatments can affect the plan

Active acne, significant irritation, infection, a tendency to raised scars or recent treatment may change timing and approach. Share all acne medicines and procedures you currently use or have used in recent years. Do not stop a medicine on your own to “prepare” your skin; discuss that with the prescriber and responsible practitioner. Raised scars or keloids need a different assessment from atrophic depressions; neither this comparison nor a photograph can confirm which category applies.

07 · Useful questions to bring to consultation

Turn the comparison into an individual decision

Ask: What type of mark or scar is being assessed? Is the acne controlled? Which exact device and mode are proposed? Does the cited evidence match this procedure and indication? How do pigmentation history, healing and medicines affect the discussion? What alternatives are available, including waiting or not treating? Which aftercare instructions and contact signs will be provided in writing? The consultation may find another approach more suitable; neither method guarantees scar removal.

08 · References and limits

References and limits

  • Comparing fractional CO₂ laser and needling-based modalities in facial acne scar treatment: a 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis
  • Randomized study of fractional CO₂ laser, microneedling and PRP for post-acne scarring (2020)
  • American Academy of Dermatology: acne scar consultation and treatment

These studies concern particular devices, protocols and populations. The 2026 review reports substantial heterogeneity and does not establish equivalence or predict results from the clinic’s unconfirmed CO₂ device or microneedling protocol.
