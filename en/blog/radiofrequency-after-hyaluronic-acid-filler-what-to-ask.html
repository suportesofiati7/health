Radiofrequency after hyaluronic acid filler: is there a safe interval?

Evidence on radiofrequency after hyaluronic acid filler is limited and procedure-specific. Learn what two studies examined and what details to confirm before choosing timing.

Why the answer depends on the exact procedure

“Radiofrequency” can mean different devices and methods. Noninvasive monopolar treatment and microneedling radiofrequency do not deliver energy in the same way. Filler location, product, treatment area, device settings, and timing also matter. A single waiting period cannot be inferred from the treatment name alone.

What the small studies actually examined

A 2007 randomized study evaluated hyaluronic acid filler combined with radiofrequency, laser, or intense pulsed light in nasolabial folds. It reported no statistically significant difference in wrinkle severity or global aesthetic scores between filler alone and the combination groups at the assessed time points. It does not establish safety for every device, technique, area, or treatment schedule.

A 2022 clinicohistological study involved 14 volunteers with abdominal filler and one specific monopolar radiofrequency protocol applied on the same day, 14 days later, or 28 days later. The histological filler score decreased in five people in the same-day group and one person in each later group. This small study in abdominal tissue cannot set a universal interval for facial filler or other devices.

Questions to take to the consultation

  • What filler was used, when, and in which area?
  • Which radiofrequency method and device are being considered: noninvasive or microneedling, monopolar or another configuration?
  • Does the planned treatment overlap the filler area, and what does the device guidance say?
  • Who will confirm timing based on the product record and the exact local protocol?

Bring the product card or procedure record if available. Do not use a study involving another device or body area to choose your own treatment date.

References and limits

  • Goldman et al. (2007), randomized evaluation of energy-based procedures after hyaluronic acid filler.
  • Jurairattanaporn et al. (2022), clinicohistological study of monopolar radiofrequency and abdominal hyaluronic acid filler.

The exact device, filler, and local protocol need practitioner confirmation. Current evidence does not establish one interval for everyone.
