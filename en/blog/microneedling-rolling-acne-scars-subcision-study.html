Rolling acne scars and microneedling: what changes when subcision is added?

One study compared microneedling alone with a combination that included subcision. Learn why study design, scar type and technique limit generalization.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational evidence review; subcision is not confirmed as a local service.

01 · “Acne scars” includes different shapes

A scar assessment asks more than whether texture is uneven

Atrophic acne scars can include ice-pick, boxcar and rolling patterns. The American Academy of Dermatology notes that scar type affects treatment planning; ice-pick scars may need a different approach from rolling scars. A photograph or a person’s own label cannot reliably determine the scar type or the right procedure.

If acne is still active, dermatologists often address breakouts before scar procedures, since new inflammation can create new scars. A clinician can also distinguish true texture scars from flat colour marks left after acne.

02 · Microneedling and subcision are not the same procedure

They address tissue in different ways

Microneedling creates controlled punctures that stimulate collagen and elastin production. Subcision is a separate procedure that releases fibrous bands pulling some depressed scars downward. Combining procedures in one study does not mean standard microneedling itself releases those attachments, nor that every rolling scar needs subcision.

The clinic service page describes microneedling. It does not list subcision, and no local combination or scar-specific indication is confirmed.

03 · What one comparative study found

Forty enrolled; 37 completed six months

A 2021 comparative study enrolled 40 people and assigned them alternately—not by random allocation—to microneedling alone or microneedling combined with subcision. Thirty-seven completed six months. The authors reported a larger decrease in rolling-scar counts in the combined group, but the groups differed in their baseline scar counts and the allocation method can introduce bias.

For total scar counts, the paper reported reductions of 4.83% and 19.65%, respectively, without a statistically significant difference. It also reported post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in one participant. These findings are preliminary and do not establish a treatment effect for a particular person or clinic.

04 · What this evidence cannot answer

A combination result is not a result for needling alone

The study compared two different treatment plans; it cannot isolate the contribution of subcision from other differences between the groups. Its small sample, non-random allocation, short follow-up and baseline imbalance limit confidence. Other studies may use different devices, techniques, scar types and outcome scales.

The paper is not evidence that the local microneedling service includes subcision or is suitable for rolling scars. It also does not establish a universal indication, a number of sessions or an individual outcome.

05 · Questions to take to a scar assessment

Clarify the concern and the exact technique

  • Are the marks flat colour changes or depressed texture scars?
  • What scar pattern has a clinician identified, and is acne currently active?
  • Is the proposal microneedling alone or a separate combination procedure?
  • What evidence matches the device, method and scar type being discussed?
  • What are the alternatives, pigment risks, recovery considerations and follow-up plan?

Do not try to perform needling or subcision at home. Research protocols describe clinician-led studies, not instructions for self-treatment.
