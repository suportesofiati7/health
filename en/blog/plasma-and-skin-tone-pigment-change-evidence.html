Plasma and skin discoloration: what does the evidence tell us?

Darker or lighter marks can appear after inflammation or skin injury. But “plasma” covers different techniques and devices: studies of one type cannot predict what another will do or describe a specific clinic’s configuration.

In brief

Tell the practitioner about prior color changes, raised scars and reactions to procedures; ask which evidence applies to the exact modality being proposed.

What pigment change means

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation is darkening that may follow inflammation or skin injury; hypopigmentation is an area that becomes lighter. Possibility and duration vary. A history of marks after acne, burns or procedures is useful context for an appointment, not a diagnosis or an individual prediction.

Plasma studies are not interchangeable

A 2020 clinical report described periorbital hyperpigmentation after plasma exeresis. A small 2025 study assessed ionized plasma in ten people with dermatosis papulosa nigra and recorded hyperpigmentation in 20% and hypopigmentation in 10% at four weeks. These are different modalities, indications, sites and study designs. Those figures are not a risk rate for plasma exeresis, any skin tone or a local device.

Skin tone does not replace history and assessment

A systematic review of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation treatments in people with darker skin found heterogeneous evidence across modalities, including reports of worsening with some treatments. It did not specifically evaluate every device called plasma. Skin tone, personal pigment history, treatment site, lesion diagnosis and proposed technique should not be reduced to a universal rule.

Useful questions to bring

  • What is the technical name of the modality: plasma exeresis, ionized plasma or another method?
  • Which manufacturer, model and registered intended purpose apply to the device?
  • What evidence is available for this modality, indication and site, including dark marks, light marks or scarring?
  • How does my history of pigment change or scarring affect assessment?
  • What alternatives, limits and contact signs should be discussed before deciding?
If a lesion has not been assessed or has changed in appearance, speak with a qualified professional before any procedure that destroys or alters it.

Sources and limits

  • Report of periorbital hyperpigmentation after plasma exeresis (2020).
  • Study of ionized plasma for dermatosis papulosa nigra (10 participants; 2025).
  • Systematic review of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation treatments in people with darker skin (2024).
These publications do not identify the locally offered plasma device, indication or protocol. The practitioner needs to confirm those details and the device’s regulatory status.
