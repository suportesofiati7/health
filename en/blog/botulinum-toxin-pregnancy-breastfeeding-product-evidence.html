Botulinum toxin in pregnancy and breastfeeding: why product and purpose matter

Pregnancy and breastfeeding are different situations, and products may have different labeling. See what research and an Anvisa communication can support—and what still needs confirmation.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; decisions depend on assessment and the specific product

This article organizes evidence and questions for a conversation with your care team. It does not recommend starting or stopping treatment, replace the current Brazilian product label, or assume which product the clinic uses.

01 · Start by identifying the situation

Pregnancy, trying to conceive and breastfeeding need separate consideration

A question about exposure during pregnancy is not the same as deciding about an elective cosmetic procedure. Breastfeeding also has a different evidence base. Before interpreting any study or guidance, it helps to know the indication, exact product, planned dose, exposure date and reproductive situation. This page cannot assess those details for an individual.

02 · Pregnancy studies have limits

No clear harm signal does not prove safety for cosmetic use

A recent systematic review included 18 studies and 486 pregnancies exposed to onabotulinumtoxinA for different indications. The studies were heterogeneous, and the authors concluded that the evidence is insufficient to establish safety during pregnancy. Therapeutic cases and unplanned exposures are not automatically equivalent to an elective cosmetic procedure. The findings cannot predict an individual outcome or establish that different brands and formulations are interchangeable.

03 · One product label does not represent every brand

Anvisa issued a product-specific safety communication about Botulift

In a safety communication to health professionals, Anvisa reproduces a contraindication for Botulift in pregnant women, people planning pregnancy and people who are breastfeeding. That information is specific to the product and labeling identified in the communication. It does not identify the product used by the clinic and should not automatically be applied to every botulinum toxin. The current approved label matching the proposed product must be checked before reaching a conclusion.

04 · Breastfeeding has its own evidence, which remains limited

Data about one formulation do not answer for all others

The LactMed database, revised in September 2026, reports that among therapeutic botulinum toxins, only onabotulinumtoxinA has been studied during breastfeeding, including small samples after cosmetic doses. Limited reports and observational studies did not identify adverse effects in some followed groups, but this does not prove absence of risk, cover every formulation or replace the Brazilian product label. Do not turn these findings into personal advice or a rule for the clinic.

05 · Cosmetic procedures and medical treatment are different questions

The indication and expected benefit change the risk discussion

The literature includes people treated for medical conditions and people exposed for cosmetic reasons; doses, needs and alternatives may differ. A finding in a therapeutic context does not establish the benefit-risk balance for an elective intervention. Decisions should consider the purpose of treatment, the current product label, advice from the medical team and applicable professional rules.

06 · If pregnancy is discovered after an injection

Record the details and seek individual guidance

Note the brand and manufacturer, if available, the date, indication and where you received treatment. Contact the responsible practitioner and the team caring for the pregnancy for advice based on the actual product and your history. Do not use an article to estimate personal risk, start measures on your own or make decisions about the pregnancy. Seek urgent care for significant symptoms or when directed by a health professional.

07 · Questions for an informed conversation

Confirm the product, purpose and source of the guidance

  • What is the exact product name, and which current Brazilian label applies?
  • Is my situation pregnancy, trying to conceive, breastfeeding or a past exposure?
  • Do the cited data study the same formulation, dose and purpose being proposed?
  • Is treatment elective or for a medical need? What alternatives are relevant?
  • Who should be involved in the decision, and how should a past exposure be documented?

08 · References and limits

Sources reviewed

  • Systematic review of onabotulinumtoxinA during pregnancy (2026; 18 studies, 486 pregnancies)
  • LactMed: therapeutic botulinum toxins and breastfeeding (revised September 15, 2026)
  • Anvisa: Botulift safety communication to health professionals (2024)

The cited studies cannot predict individual safety. The Botulift label must not be generalized to another product. Confirm the current Anvisa-approved professional label for the exact medicine and seek individual guidance from the responsible care team.
