PREPARATION GUIDE / LASER AND IPL

Tattoos or permanent makeup before laser or IPL: what to disclose

Tell the team about decorative tattoos, micropigmentation, eyeliner or camouflage pigment before light-based treatment. Pigment in the area may change the assessment, even when it is not the treatment target.

Before your session

Show every area that may receive light

  1. Disclose tattoos and permanent makeup.
  2. Confirm laser, IPL or another modality.
  3. Ask how pigment will be handled.

Include cosmetic pigment in your history

Disclose decorative tattoos, eyeliner, eyebrow or lip pigmentation, micropigmentation and camouflage pigment used over scars. Share where they are, how long ago they were done and whether there was a reaction, fading or recent touch-up. Photos can help locate partly covered pigment, but the assessment must consider the skin in person.

Do not assume a small, light, old or off-center tattoo is irrelevant. The type of light, target, distance from the area and device instructions all affect the decision.

Light may interact with pigment even when that is not the goal

Laser and IPL are different sources, and each applicator and wavelength has its own purpose. Tattoo inks and cosmetic pigments may absorb certain light ranges. ARPANSA provider guidance lists decorative and cosmetic tattooing in a treatment area among the factors to assess, and notes that intentional tattoo removal requires specific types of lasers.

This Australian safety guidance is a reference, not a Brazilian rule or the manual for the local Harmony platform. The clinic must confirm which source, applicator and indication it proposes and follow instructions applicable to that exact model.

Tattoo removal is a separate indication

Laser systems for tattoo removal select wavelengths to interact with specific ink colors. That does not mean an IPL platform intended for another purpose removes tattoos; health authorities warn that IPL is not intended for ink removal and may cause injury if used outside its intended purpose.

Do not ask the team to “go over” pigment, or try to cover it with makeup or a dressing for the session without instructions. Ask which area will be treated, what the manufacturer says about tattoos and cosmetic pigment, and whether the proposal needs to be changed, limited or postponed.

Questions to ask before consenting

  • What is the treatment goal, and which modality, device, applicator and filter will be used?
  • How does tattooing, micropigmentation or permanent makeup in the area affect eligibility?
  • Does the manual for the model used by the clinic give specific instructions for pigment in the treatment field?
  • How will the team identify and manage pigmented areas without promising that any covering method is safe?
  • Which signs after the session warrant contact or medical assessment, and which channel should I use?

If the device or how pigment in the area will be handled is unclear, wait until the team explains the plan before starting. A platform with several applicators does not make its modalities interchangeable.

References and limits

  • ARPANSA: laser and IPL safety advice for providers — includes cosmetic and decorative tattooing in the treatment area in risk assessment; Australian reference, not a local protocol.
  • Queensland Health: tattoo removal — explains that removal requires specific lasers and IPL is not intended for that purpose.
  • FDA: tattoo removal options and results — US source on removal, risks and limits of do-it-yourself methods; it does not confirm Brazilian indications.

The modality, model, applicator, filter and local instructions for tattoos need confirmation by the responsible practitioner before treatment. This content does not determine eligibility or replace an individual assessment.
