Postpartum hair shedding: what to assess before considering injections

Noticing more shedding a few months after childbirth may reflect a temporary change in the hair cycle. The pattern, timing and other signs help determine whether dermatology assessment should come before discussing a procedure.

When postpartum shedding may begin

The American Academy of Dermatology notes that many people notice increased shedding a few months after childbirth, often peaking around four months. The British Association of Dermatologists explains that telogen effluvium can begin about three months after a trigger such as childbirth and often improves over several months. These are general reference points, not a guaranteed timeline for an individual.

Telogen effluvium commonly causes diffuse shedding: more hair in the shower or brush and reduced volume across the scalp rather than one clearly defined patch. Regaining visible fullness can take longer than the period of heavier shedding.

Diffuse shedding does not confirm the cause

Childbirth is one possible trigger, but hair loss can also relate to other conditions, medicines, thyroid changes, iron deficiency or more than one factor at once. Itching, pain, significant scaling, patchy loss, eyebrow loss or a progressively widening area deserve assessment rather than automatic attribution to the postpartum period.

Dermatology professionals may combine medical history with scalp examination. Blood tests or other tools are considered according to the findings; there is no single list that everyone needs.

Before deciding about scalp mesotherapy

An injectable technique does not identify the cause of shedding by itself, and mesotherapy research studies different products, routes and protocols. Evidence for one ingredient or indication does not automatically validate another formulation. If you are breastfeeding or taking medicines, confirm the exact product, route, indication, known risks and uncertainties with the professionals overseeing your health; do not assume a cosmetic procedure is safe because it is local.

  • What possibility explains the observed pattern, and who assessed it?
  • What would change the decision from monitoring to investigating other causes?
  • Which substance and concentration would be used, by what route, and what evidence applies to this condition?
  • How do recent pregnancy, breastfeeding, medicines and other health conditions affect the assessment?
  • Which outcome will be followed, and when will the proposal be reviewed?

Do not start, stop or change medicines and supplements on your own. This page does not diagnose postpartum hair loss or recommend mesotherapy.
