Hair mesotherapy and minoxidil: why does the route change the evidence?

“Mesotherapy” does not identify a substance or indication. Some trials studied intradermal minoxidil for androgenetic alopecia; that does not establish that the same active ingredient, concentration or protocol is used locally, or that it applies to every cause of hair loss.

One ingredient, one route and one diagnostic question

Evidence for a product applied to the scalp does not automatically transfer to an injected solution. Route, concentration, formulation, interval, technique and diagnosis are part of the research question. “Hair mesotherapy” can refer to different substances and combinations; results from one trial do not validate all such products or other causes of alopecia.

What two trials of intradermal minoxidil assessed

A single-blind randomized trial published in 2026 compared 0.5% mesodermal minoxidil injections with 2% topical minoxidil in 40 women diagnosed with female androgenetic alopecia. At 16 weeks, mean hair-density increase was 8.70 hairs/cm² in the injection group and 4.50 hairs/cm² in the topical group. Physicians rated greater improvement in the injection group, but participants’ self-assessments did not differ significantly. The study had only 20 participants per group and does not answer questions about other diagnoses, substances, formulations or long-term follow-up.

An earlier placebo-controlled trial included 54 women with the same diagnosis and compared intradermal 0.5% minoxidil injections with saline. It reported changes in hair measures and self-assessments under the studied protocol. A trial in 49 men compared topical 5% minoxidil with a mesotherapy solution administered in eight sessions alongside microneedling. That combination does not isolate one ingredient’s effect: substance, route and mechanical injury all enter the comparison.

  • Placebo-controlled trial of intradermal minoxidil in women (2021)
  • Trial of mesotherapy with microneedling versus topical minoxidil in men (2019)

What the systematic review adds — and its limits

A 2022 systematic review included 12 studies of mesotherapy for pattern hair loss, with 527 participants. Included studies tested varied approaches; the authors noted that samples were not large and comparisons with other treatments were insufficient. The review’s search ended in January 2021, so it does not include the 2026 trial cited above. A review of heterogeneous protocols cannot answer which product, route or benefit applies to one person or one service.

Tang et al., systematic review of mesotherapy for pattern hair loss (2022).

What to confirm before considering a product

  • What is the working diagnosis for the hair loss, and who assessed it?
  • Which ingredient, concentration, formulation and route are proposed?
  • Is the product authorized for that presentation and route? Which official information or label applies?
  • Which study matches the proposed diagnosis, ingredient, dose, route and protocol?
  • Which alternatives, adverse effects, costs, follow-up and reasons to stop should be discussed?

Do not start, inject, mix or stop medication based on a webpage or a study of a different formulation. The clinic does not publicly identify the ingredients in its service; the composition, indication, product, route and professional scope must be confirmed by the responsible practitioner.
