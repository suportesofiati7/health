Forehead, glabella and eye area: why assess movement together?

A discussion about expression lines starts with movement and goals, not an automatic list of injection points.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Observe expression in context

The face moves as a coordinated whole

Lines may appear or deepen when someone frowns, raises the brows or smiles. The AAD explains that botulinum toxin acts on specific muscles and that a plan depends on area and goal. This does not mean every area should be treated at once: assessment should clarify which movements or lines matter to the person and what they hope to preserve.

02 · Label information is not an individual plan

Indications and combinations differ by product

The US BOTOX Cosmetic label includes studies of forehead lines with concurrent glabellar treatment for that product and protocol. This does not define a universal plan, confirm which product is used in Brazil or replace the current label for the local product. Care also depends on anatomy, history and individual priorities.

03 · Priorities and limitations

“Natural” needs a concrete discussion

Discuss which expressions you hope to soften and which you want to keep. Ask what the proposal may change, what may remain and which risks or alternatives belong in the discussion. Perfect symmetry cannot be guaranteed, and an individual response cannot be predicted from a photograph.

04 · Useful consultation questions

Bring observations, not a point-by-point prescription

  • Which movements make the lines appear?
  • Which area bothers me most, and which expression do I want to preserve?
  • How do brow and eyelid position factor into assessment?
  • Which product is being considered, and what does its current label say?
  • Which effects, limitations and follow-up instructions should I understand?

Sources and limits

What these sources can help explain

The AAD and FDA label explain general concepts and one US product example; they do not establish the locally used product, indication, dose, areas or protocol. The practitioner must confirm the current local label and validate practice and follow-up statements.
