Microdermabrasion: what should you report if your skin worsens?

Mild redness and temporary sensitivity can occur, but worsening symptoms, severe pain or persistent changes deserve a conversation with your clinician. Learn what to notice without trying to diagnose yourself.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

A mild reaction can occur: notice the trend

After microdermabrasion, skin may look pink or red, feel slightly swollen or sensitive for a short time. Clinical sources describe somewhat different durations; that does not create a timeline that applies to everyone or every device. Notice how the sensation changes and follow the instructions given for your procedure.

When to contact the clinician

The Aesthetic Society advises contacting the clinician for severe or prolonged pain, noticeable redness or swelling. Burning, bruising and sun sensitivity also appear in AAD materials as possible effects. Blisters, open skin, discharge, signs of infection or clear worsening should not be assessed from a webpage alone: ask a qualified professional. Severe symptoms or symptoms affecting your general wellbeing may need medical care.

What to tell the care team

Explain when symptoms began, which area is affected, whether they are improving or worsening, and what you applied afterward. Mention sun exposure, friction or another product, without stopping prescribed medicines on your own. If useful and comfortable, a photograph in similar lighting may help describe a change, but it does not replace an assessment.

Before leaving the appointment

Ask for written post-procedure instructions and confirm whom to contact if your skin worsens, including what to do outside regular hours. Do not add exfoliants or home remedies to “correct” a reaction without checking with the care team. This information does not define the clinic’s protocol or identify the device used.
