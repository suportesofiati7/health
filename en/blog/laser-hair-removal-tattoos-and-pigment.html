Laser hair removal and tattoos: what to disclose before treatment

Tattoos and permanent makeup in a treatment area are important details to disclose. Learn what the evidence can and cannot say and what to ask about the device and area.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Show the exact area before planning

Ink changes the discussion about the treatment area

Tell the practitioner if there is a tattoo, permanent makeup or other ink-based pigmentation in the area where you are considering laser hair removal. Describe the location and show the area during assessment. A webpage cannot determine whether a specific area should be treated, avoided or handled differently: this depends on the device, manufacturer instructions, pigmentation and professional plan.

02 · What a case report can—and cannot—tell us

One burn case cannot predict individual risk

A published case report described a second-degree burn within a tattoo after intense pulsed light hair removal. One case cannot tell us how often this occurs or predict what will happen with another device, ink, skin or technique. It is a reason to discuss pigmentation and not assume every light source behaves the same way in every area.

03 · Do not confuse photoepilation with tattoo removal

Hair and tattoo removal have different goals

Laser hair removal and tattoo removal have different purposes and use different devices and protocols; they should not be confused. Evidence or parameters from one do not authorise the other. Clinical literature about tattoos and light includes adverse-event reports, but it does not establish a protocol for the clinic’s LightSheer Duet or for any individual.

04 · Ask for guidance for the actual device

Disclose tattoos, permanent makeup and recent changes

Before treatment, ask how the device and its intended use relate to the pigmented area, which manufacturer instructions will be followed, whether the area will be excluded or assessed another way, and whom to contact if a reaction occurs. Also mention raised scars, recent changes in the tattoo or previous reactions. Do not hide the ink or try to cover it with a household product for the session.
