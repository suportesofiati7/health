Melasma or post-inflammatory pigmentation: why the cause changes the conversation

Melasma and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation can be confused, but their contexts differ. Learn why identifying the cause matters before discussing a pigmentation protocol.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Melasma and PIH can look similar

“Dark spot” describes an appearance, not a diagnosis

Melasma and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) can create darker areas, but they are not the same condition. PIH often appears after inflammation or injury, such as acne or irritation. Melasma is a distinct pigmentary condition and may be associated with factors such as sunlight, hormones and other triggers. Their appearance can overlap; a web search or photograph cannot confirm the cause.

02 · Prior inflammation and other triggers are clues, not proof

History helps distinguish different contexts

Note when the marks began, whether they followed inflammation, how they change over time and which medicines, products, sun exposure or procedures came before them. These details help a consultation but are not a home diagnostic test. A new spot, a changing lesion or one that differs from others should be assessed appropriately before being treated as a cosmetic mark.

03 · Depth, skin tone and recurrence belong in assessment

Evidence does not point to one universal protocol

AAD dermatology guidance describes melasma assessment as considering differential diagnosis, skin tone, apparent depth and triggers; there is no single best treatment for everyone. With PIH, preventing further inflammation and photoprotection may also be relevant. US references help explain these concepts but do not confirm the products, ingredients or steps of a Brazilian clinic’s protocol.

04 · Confirm the working diagnosis before choosing a plan

Questions that help prepare for assessment

Ask which explanation fits the change, what still needs to be ruled out, whether the priority is controlling inflammation, protecting the skin or discussing pigment treatment, and how recurrence will be monitored. Do not combine active ingredients or procedures based only on a spot’s name.
