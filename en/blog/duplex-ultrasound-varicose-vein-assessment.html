Duplex ultrasound for varicose vein assessment: what it does

Duplex ultrasound can help assess venous flow in selected situations. Learn why it is not an automatic requirement for every visible small vein and cannot define an aesthetic proposal by itself.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · The test combines imaging and information about flow

Duplex is a flow assessment, not a treatment

Venous duplex ultrasound combines images of veins with information about blood flow. It can help locate reflux and inform assessment of certain venous conditions. It does not remove visible vessels, replace a consultation or determine by itself which procedure is appropriate for an individual.

02 · Symptoms and larger veins can change assessment

Its use depends on the clinical question

Vein-society guidelines for lower-extremity varicose veins include duplex in the evaluation of defined cases of varicose veins, together with history and clinical examination. Assessment may consider larger veins, symptoms, signs and the question a clinician needs to answer. This is different from saying that everyone with small surface vessels needs the test.

03 · Evidence must be kept within its scope

A varicose-vein guideline is not a rule for every small vessel

A guideline from US vascular societies applies to the population and context it describes; it does not establish a Brazilian PEIM rule or this clinic’s workflow. The responsible health professional should decide whether testing is needed based on symptoms, clinical findings, diagnostic questions and local standards. An aesthetic procedure name cannot establish venous disease or whether imaging is necessary.

04 · Ask who orders it and how results will be used

How to discuss whether testing is needed

Ask what clinical question duplex would answer, who will assess you, what the result might change and whether testing relates to symptoms or another finding. If you have relevant symptoms or skin changes, seek vascular assessment before making an aesthetic decision. Read about PEIM for visible small vessels and when appearance and symptoms call for broader assessment.
