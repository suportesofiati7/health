Jessner peel and melasma: what do studies actually support?

Two small trials studied Jessner peel for melasma in different contexts. Learn what they compared, what they cannot establish and why the evidence does not define an individual protocol.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; indication requires professional assessment

Peel studies do not define an individual plan

Melasma is a recurrent pigmentary condition, and peel studies investigate specific formulas, comparators and follow-up periods. Asking what was studied helps distinguish a research result from a recommendation for an individual or from a clinic’s protocol.

Jessner solution compared with salicylic acid

A randomized, double-blind trial published in 2008 included 60 people with epidermal melasma in Asian skin. It compared Jessner solution with 30% salicylic acid; both groups also received sunscreen and moisturizer instructions. After 12 weeks of treatment and another 12 weeks of follow-up, the between-group difference in MASI was not statistically significant. Its design and population limit how far the findings can be transferred.

Jessner combined with TCA is a different question

In a randomized split-face trial, 24 adult women with skin phototypes IV–V received six sessions at two-week intervals, comparing 20–25% TCA alone with TCA combined with Jessner solution. MASI reduction was greater on the combination side. Because the study added Jessner to TCA, it does not show that Jessner alone would produce the same result; it also does not establish safety or efficacy for every phototype, formula or service.

Heterogeneity prevents a simple ranking

A 2024 systematic review included 24 chemical-peel studies for melasma, with 1,075 participants and study durations of 8 to 36 weeks. The methods varied too much for the authors to conduct a meta-analysis. The review does not automatically validate a specific Jessner protocol. During a consultation, ask what diagnosis is being considered, which outcome will be followed, what alternatives exist and how maintenance and recurrence enter the discussion. Do not combine or apply peels on your own.

Studies and limitations

These studies assess specific populations, formulas and combinations. None identifies the local product or protocol or predicts an individual outcome.
