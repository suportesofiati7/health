Radiofrequency for firmness: how should you read the evidence?

Learn the difference between a proposed mechanism, a clinical outcome and a promise when reviewing radiofrequency studies for skin firmness.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational reading

What did a study actually evaluate?

“Radiofrequency” is a broad category. Studies may examine different devices, modalities, body areas, goals and comparators. A 2021 systematic review found 25 randomized trials published between 2009 and 2019 across varied indications; the authors noted study-quality problems, including selection, performance and detection bias. They considered the technology promising but did not find enough evidence to justify preferring it over other available options.

A proposed mechanism is not the same as a visible change

An explanation involving tissue heating or collagen remodeling does not by itself answer how much appearance may change, for whom, for how long, or compared with what alternative. To interpret a result, look at the measured outcome, sample size, follow-up, comparator, reported adverse effects and study funding.

One comparative study does not apply to every device

A 2025 prospective trial compared radiofrequency with focused ultrasound in 36 participants at a single center, without randomization, using specific device models. Its design and sample limit generalization. Even a well-described study does not establish efficacy, safety or settings for a different device, or confirm the device used in a local service.

Questions to ask before considering radiofrequency

Ask for the exact model and modality; clarify which goal is being assessed and whether studies used the same device, indication and body area. Discuss alternatives, realistic limits, individual variation, risks, recovery and how the care team will assess response. The practitioner should confirm what is available and which documentation applies to the protocol.

Sources and limits

This guide helps readers interpret studies; it does not determine suitability for an individual. These publications do not confirm the clinic’s radiofrequency modality, device model, settings or approved claims.
