Retinoic acid peels and melasma: what did a Brazilian trial examine?

A Brazilian trial compared three retinoic acid peel formulations for melasma. Learn what it found, who was excluded and why it does not define a clinic’s protocol.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational study appraisal; it does not recommend a peel or establish the local protocol.

01 · What the trial compared

Three groups, with twenty participants each

This Brazilian randomized, double-blind trial enrolled 60 people with melasma and assigned 20 to each of three groups: a conventional 1% retinoic acid peel, a 1% retinoic acid peel in a microemulsion vehicle, or placebo. The study compared changes in the Melasma Area and Severity Index (MASI) and a melasma quality-of-life questionnaire over 60 days.

Participants received four sessions at 15-day intervals. Those details describe the research protocol; they are not instructions or a schedule for someone considering a peel.

02 · How to read the reported result

The formulation mattered in this particular study

The authors reported a greater reduction in pigmentation scores with the microemulsion formulation than with the conventional vehicle or placebo. All three groups showed a significant MASI reduction from baseline, and the quality-of-life score also improved across groups. A result within one short trial does not show that every retinoic acid peel, formulation or melasma pattern has the same effect.

The trial compared specific 1% formulations. It did not compare the local clinic’s product or establish that the service page uses either study formulation.

03 · Who was not represented

Eligibility limits matter when applying evidence

The trial excluded, among others, people with Fitzpatrick phototypes V and VI, pregnant or breastfeeding participants, and those planning pregnancy in the following three months. It also excluded several health, medication and recent-procedure situations. These are study eligibility rules, not a complete contraindication list or universal clinical guidance.

Because the follow-up ended at 60 days, this trial cannot establish long-term melasma control, recurrence, rare adverse effects or safety for groups it excluded. Discuss personal history and treatment questions with a qualified clinician.

04 · Questions before considering a retinoic peel

Confirm the exact formulation and the purpose of care

  • What exact agent, formulation and intended use are being proposed?
  • Is the goal melasma, another pigment concern or something else, and who has assessed that diagnosis?
  • Does the study match the product, skin presentation and population being discussed?
  • What are the realistic limits, alternatives, pigment risks and follow-up plan?
  • What should someone disclose about pregnancy or breastfeeding, medicines, recent procedures and skin reactions?

Do not start, stop or apply prescription retinoids or other active products based on a study summary. Follow an individualized assessment and the written instructions for the actual product and procedure.

05 · Source and limits

One trial informs a question; it does not establish local practice

This randomized trial is directly relevant to one retinoic acid microemulsion peel for melasma, but its sample and 60-day observation period are limited. The clinic’s retinoic peel page does not name its exact product, concentration or protocol. No claim is made that the local service uses the trial formulation.
