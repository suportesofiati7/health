Ultraformer MPT and facial volume: questions about treatment goals

Microfocused ultrasound studies examine different devices and areas. Learn why contour, firmness and volume should be discussed separately before planning treatment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Say whether your priority is firmness, contour or preserving volume

Contour and firmness are not the same goal

Someone may want greater firmness while also wanting to preserve volume in certain areas. “Lifting,” “contour” and “fat reduction” are not interchangeable goals: the objective, anatomy of the area and device used all affect what is being studied or proposed. Describe the changes you want and those you hope to avoid, especially if your main concern is facial-volume loss.

02 · MFU-V research is not automatically research on Ultraformer MPT

Studies do not represent one device or protocol

The literature includes different devices, applicators, focal depths, treatment areas and outcome measures. A review of microfocused ultrasound with visualization (MFU-V), for example, examined mainly data from another system, not the protocol used at this clinic. It identified rare significant adverse events in published articles and surveillance reports, but those records do not measure risk rates for Ultraformer MPT or prove that a particular outcome will occur with it.

03 · One device study does not predict what will happen to your face

Volume changes require anatomical context

Published studies that name Ultraformer MPT still apply to the population, area, mode and design they examined. One investigation of 50 participants measured facial changes immediately after treatment; another examined submental fat reduction and followed 30 people for four weeks. These findings can inform questions, but they do not establish suitability for other areas, lasting effects or outcomes for your anatomy.

04 · Ask about area, intent and limits

Questions to align the plan with your priority

Ask what goal is proposed for each area, whether the plan aims to change adipose tissue, how weight and volume history enter the assessment, what alternatives exist and what the evidence actually supports. The practitioner should confirm the device, applicator, plan and manufacturer instructions before describing an individual protocol.
