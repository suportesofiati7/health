Skin laxity, volume or contour: what is the main concern?

Learn why firmness, volume and contour are different goals, and which questions can help assess a proposal.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Name the concern

“Laxity” can describe different perceptions

Someone may notice looser skin, reduced volume, a change in contour, or a combination. The word used to describe appearance does not, by itself, identify the structure involved or determine which procedure is appropriate. Assessment starts by understanding what changed, where, and what the person hopes will change.

02 · Firmness is not the same as volume

Skin-firming options do not replace an assessment of volume

The American Academy of Dermatology describes different approaches to sagging skin and notes that nonsurgical procedures may produce modest, variable results depending on the option used. This is general information about treatment categories, not evidence specific to Ultraformer MPT and not a promise that a contour will be lifted or reshaped. The public service page does not establish that every volume or contour goal can be addressed with this device.

03 · Turn the goal into a specific question

Compare the proposed goal with evidence for the exact device

Ask which observed feature is being considered, what change would be realistic, and which alternatives or limitations belong in the discussion. Studies of a different technology or brand do not automatically answer questions about the device used at a clinic. Suitability also depends on history, anatomy, area, and documentation that applies to the exact model and applicator.

04 · Questions to bring to consultation

A useful conversation separates the goal, assessment and proposal

  • Is my main concern skin firmness, volume, contour, or another change?
  • What in the assessment supports the proposal for this area?
  • What would be unrealistic to expect from the device?
  • What alternatives exist, including no procedure for now?
  • Which model and applicator would be used, and what documentation describes its intended purpose?

05 · Sources and limits

What these sources can help explain

The AAD describes general options for sagging skin and emphasizes professional assessment; its page does not validate a specific device available at this clinic. The cited FDA guidance concerns device regulation and safety in the United States; it does not establish Brazilian authorization or identify the local model.

  • American Academy of Dermatology: many ways to firm sagging skin
  • FDA: guidance for focused ultrasound systems for aesthetic use (United States)
