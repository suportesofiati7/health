Oral isotretinoin and deep cleansing: questions before extraction

If you take oral isotretinoin, tell the person assessing a cleanse before scheduling. Comedone extraction, abrasion and waxing are different procedures, and your skin may be more sensitive during treatment.

Isotretinoin can make skin drier and more sensitive

Dryness, irritation and increased skin sensitivity are relevant during oral isotretinoin use. Tell both the team that manages your medicine and the person assessing the cleanse that you are in treatment. Also share the dose, duration, recent changes, current skin symptoms and any other products or procedures you use.

Do not stop, reduce or change isotretinoin to make an aesthetic procedure possible. Medication decisions belong to the prescriber; an elective procedure can be postponed while the teams clarify risks and care.

“Cleansing” can include different steps

A session may involve washing, emollient preparation, selective manual extraction, mechanical exfoliation, microdermabrasion, devices or other products. The term “deep cleanse” does not tell you which steps will be used. Ask for the plan and confirm whether it involves pressure, abrasion, puncturing or applying substances to the skin.

Also distinguish comedone extraction from dermabrasion, microdermabrasion or waxing. These interventions differ in depth, mechanism and evidence. Guidance for one does not automatically answer whether another is suitable.

What guidelines say about extraction during isotretinoin

The American Academy of Dermatology’s 2024 acne guideline concludes that available evidence is insufficient to recommend for or against comedone extraction as an acne treatment modality. The Brazilian Society of Dermatology has published a consensus that separates procedures by category and discusses safety during isotretinoin; it does not assess every local cleansing protocol or make every extraction automatically indicated.

The AAD advises against waxing during oral isotretinoin and for six months afterward because of skin injury risk. That warning is specific to waxing and should not be turned into a six-month rule for every cleanse. A decision depends on your skin that day, the exact procedure, its purpose and the dermatologist’s plan for the medicine.

Bring these questions to your assessment

  • Which steps will be done and which are optional?
  • Will there be manual extraction, abrasion, waxing, acids or a device?
  • How do current dryness, stinging, cracks or inflammation affect the decision?
  • Should the plan be coordinated with the dermatologist who prescribes isotretinoin?
  • If the session is postponed or changed, which simple care steps suit my skin?

If you have pain, cracks, sores, crusting or significant irritation, describe it before any manipulation. The assessor may decide that no extraction, a session without extraction or postponement is more appropriate at that time.

References and limits

  • American Academy of Dermatology: acne vulgaris care guideline (2024) — finds evidence insufficient to recommend comedone extraction as a treatment modality.
  • Brazilian Society of Dermatology: consensus on oral isotretinoin — gives recommendations by procedure category; it does not replace assessment of a specific protocol.
  • American Academy of Dermatology: isotretinoin FAQs — a specific warning about waxing; it does not set timing for every cleansing procedure.

The local service’s steps, products, postponement criteria and professional scope need confirmation by the practitioner. This guide does not replace the dermatologist managing isotretinoin.
