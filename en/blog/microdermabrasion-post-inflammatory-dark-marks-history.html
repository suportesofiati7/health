Microdermabrasion and post-inflammatory dark marks: what should you share?

If acne or irritation leaves dark marks, share that history before discussing microdermabrasion. Learn what general guidance says and what needs assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · A mark is not always a scar

Describe how your skin tends to respond

After acne, inflammation or an injury, a flat mark can remain even when there is no structural scar. If your skin tends to darken after spots, bites, cuts or irritation, that pattern is useful assessment information. You do not need to arrive with a diagnosis: describe what happened and how long the mark took to fade.

02 · Share your pigment history

Past responses can change the conversation

The American Academy of Dermatology says people who develop dark spots after acne or an injury may have a greater risk of developing more dark spots after microdermabrasion. This is a reason to discuss history and options; it is not an automatic contraindication and cannot predict what will happen for one person. The guidance is general and does not establish the risk or protocol for a specific device at the clinic.

03 · What information to bring

Organize details without stopping medicines

Mention active acne, stinging, peeling or open areas; past persistent dark marks after irritation; products and medicines you use; any isotretinoin history; and recent procedures. The AAD advises discussing recent isotretinoin use and scarring risk with a dermatologist before microdermabrasion. Do not stop or change a medicine on your own.

04 · Useful questions before deciding

Understand the device and purpose of the visit

Ask about the realistic goal, which device and tip will be used, how intensity is chosen, what aftercare is expected and which changes you should report. The AAD’s general microdermabrasion guides do not identify the local model or replace manufacturer instructions and professional assessment. If a spot is new, changing or bleeding, seek dermatology assessment before treating it as a cosmetic mark.
