Laser hair removal: which medicines and products should you mention?

Bring a list of medicines, supplements and skin products to your consultation. Learn what to record and why you should not stop anything on your own.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational guide; confirm local instructions

Why the list belongs in your assessment

Some medicines, topical products and supplements may be relevant to a laser assessment. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends telling the clinician about prescription and over-the-counter medicines, including isotretinoin, as well as factors such as recent tanning and a tendency to scar.

That does not mean every medicine rules out treatment. Relevance depends on the product, the condition it treats, the area, your skin, and the device and protocol being considered. The list helps the practitioner assess your situation and check product-specific guidance when needed.

What to write down before your appointment

Record the medicine or product name, dose or strength if known, how you use it, and when you started or changed it. Include prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and supplements, creams, acids and other products applied to the area being considered.

Also note allergies or past reactions, recent procedures, tanning or sunburn near the planned date, and why you take relevant medicines. If you do not know the exact name, bring the package or a clear photo of its label. You do not need to decide whether something is “photosensitizing”; share it for review.

Do not stop medicine to “clear” yourself for laser

Do not stop, reduce or change a prescribed medicine, isotretinoin, contraception or any other treatment because of a cosmetic session without speaking with the prescriber. The laser practitioner may recommend postponing or request clarification, but changes to medical treatment should be discussed with the person who prescribed it.

If a topical product may matter, ask for specific instructions about whether to pause it, on which area and for how long. Do not apply a rule found for another device, procedure or person.

Questions that make the decision clearer

Ask: Which part of my history affects this assessment? Does the proposed laser model and mode have product-specific guidance relevant to anything on my list? If the session needs to be postponed, whom should I contact about my medicine? What written instructions apply to the treated area?

The service page identifies LightSheer Duet but does not publish a local list of medicines that require a pause or an individual protocol. Confirm this during assessment based on the device, your history and prescriber guidance when needed.
