TCA CROSS for acne scars: is it the same as a TCA peel?

TCA CROSS is a focal technique studied for atrophic scars. Learn why its studies do not establish the results of a TCA peel applied in a different way.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational study appraisal; it does not establish that the clinic offers CROSS.

01 · Same acid name, different technique

A focal scar technique is not a general peel

TCA CROSS (chemical reconstruction of skin scars) is described in research as a focal approach to selected atrophic scars. That is different from a chemical peel applied across a broader skin area. They use the same acid family, but the target, application pattern, treatment question and potential tissue effect differ.

The local ATA service page describes a chemical peel. It does not confirm that the clinic performs TCA CROSS or treats acne scars with this technique. Evidence about CROSS should not be used as proof for a general peel.

02 · What a recent randomized study compared

Forty participants, two focal-treatment groups

A randomized trial published in 2025 compared TCA CROSS at two concentrations in 40 people with atrophic acne scars, with 20 participants in each group. The researchers found no significant overall difference in patient- and researcher-rated results between groups. In the higher-concentration group, ice-pick scars improved more than boxcar scars, and crusting lasted longer.

This result highlights that scar morphology matters and that the study did not show an overall advantage for the higher concentration. It does not establish which technique is suitable for an individual, and it cannot be transferred to full-area TCA peeling.

03 · Why the evidence cannot be generalized

Study findings depend on method and scar type

Earlier studies also examined focal CROSS for specific scar patterns, including ice-pick scars. Their samples, concentrations, assessment methods and follow-up differ. A favorable result in a selected study group does not mean every atrophic scar responds alike or that a TCA peel has the same effect.

Acne scars may be atrophic, raised or discoloured, and the scar pattern changes which treatment question is relevant. A clinician should assess the diagnosis and scar type rather than relying on a photo or the word “peel.”

04 · Questions to ask before discussing an ATA peel

Clarify what procedure is actually being proposed

  • Is the proposal a chemical peel over an area or a focal scar technique such as CROSS?
  • Has a clinician assessed the acne scars and identified their pattern?
  • Does the cited study use the same technique and target the same scar type?
  • What are the risks of pigment change, prolonged crusting, worsening texture or additional scarring?
  • What alternatives and follow-up apply to the exact procedure being discussed?

Do not apply trichloroacetic acid at home or use research protocols as instructions. The concentration and application details in studies are not a consumer guide.

05 · Sources and limits

A focused-scar trial is not evidence for every ATA peel

The randomized study below informs one comparison within focal TCA CROSS for atrophic acne scars. It does not establish the efficacy of the local service, identify the local product or technique, or show that a full-area TCA peel treats atrophic acne scars.
