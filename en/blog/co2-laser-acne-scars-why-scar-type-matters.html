CO₂ laser for acne scars: why does scar type matter?

Depressed scars, raised scars and flat marks are not the same. Learn how scar type limits the evidence for CO₂ laser and what to discuss at an individual assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Evidence-informed reading

A flat mark after acne may be a color change without a structural change in contour. Atrophic scars are depressed; hypertrophic scars or keloids are raised. Each calls for a different discussion. Classification requires professional assessment, not a photograph alone.

Depressed scars have different shapes

Ice-pick scars are narrow and deep; boxcar scars form rounder or oval depressions; rolling scars are wider and shallow, with a wavy appearance. More than one pattern can coexist. These differences help frame the clinical question because one modality should not be assumed to suit every contour equally.

What the CO₂ laser review can tell us

A systematic review of ablative fractional CO₂ laser for atrophic acne scars included 30 studies: 12 retrospective studies and 18 prospective randomized clinical trials published through 2016. The authors considered the modality effective across the studies, but said sessions, intervals and parameters should be individualized; for ice-pick scars, they advised considering combination therapy. This does not identify the local configuration or prove an outcome for an individual.

How to bring the question to an assessment

Ask which type of mark is being assessed, whether acne is still active, which goal would be followed and whether the cited study matches the scar type, skin phototype and modality under discussion. The CO₂-versus-microneedling comparison summarizes another part of the evidence; the local service and protocol should be confirmed with the clinic. No page can guarantee complete scar removal.

Sources and limits

These sources describe general scar patterns and studies of specific modalities. They do not establish the configuration, protocol, indication or outcome for the local service.

05 · Recovery and discomfort cannot be inferred from the name

Ask about the actual plan being considered

There is no single recovery timeline that applies to every CO₂ laser or microneedling procedure. Treatment area, depth, settings, technique, skin and individual response matter. Study protocols and outcome measures varied; the literature cannot transfer a session calendar or recovery promise to this clinic. Before deciding, ask which exact modality would be used, what signs are expected, how to get aftercare guidance and which changes should prompt contact or medical assessment.

06 · Some situations may change the timing

Active acne and prior treatments can affect the plan

Active acne, significant irritation, infection, a tendency to raised scars or recent treatment may change timing and approach. Share all acne medicines and procedures you currently use or have used in recent years. Do not stop a medicine on your own to “prepare” your skin; discuss that with the prescriber and responsible practitioner. Raised scars or keloids need a different assessment from atrophic depressions; neither this comparison nor a photograph can confirm which category applies.

07 · Useful questions to bring to consultation

Turn the comparison into an individual decision

Ask: What type of mark or scar is being assessed? Is the acne controlled? Which exact device and mode are proposed? Does the cited evidence match this procedure and indication? How do pigmentation history, healing and medicines affect the discussion? What alternatives are available, including waiting or not treating? Which aftercare instructions and contact signs will be provided in writing? The consultation may find another approach more suitable; neither method guarantees scar removal.
