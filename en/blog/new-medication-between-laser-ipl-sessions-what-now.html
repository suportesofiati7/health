A new prescription changes the discussion before your next session

If you started, stopped or changed a medication between IPL or laser sessions, tell the clinic before returning. Your first assessment may no longer reflect your current situation. This also applies to topical medicines, supplements and products used on the treatment area—share the name and when the change happened.

Some medicines can increase skin sensitivity to light, but “photosensitizing” is not an automatic answer for every device or person. The light source, treatment area, indication, current skin condition and device instructions all matter. The Harmony platform page describes individual assessment; it does not identify the device and applicator planned for each session.

Why there is no universal waiting period

A recent clinical review of laser and energy-based procedure complications discusses concerns about photosensitizing medicines, but also notes that there is no standardized list of implicated drugs or universal discontinuation period. The review notes that through 2014, no published reports had described adverse laser reactions in people taking photosensitizing medicines. This does not prove zero risk, but helps avoid turning a precaution into a rule without considering the procedure.

ARPANSA, Australia’s radiation-protection agency, advises providers to reassess a person when relevant details change—including starting a potentially photosensitizing medicine—and considers repeating a test spot when appropriate. This is Australian provider guidance; it does not establish Brazilian regulation, a mandatory test or a waiting period for the clinic’s device.

“IPL,” “laser” and photodynamic therapy are not synonyms. Photodynamic therapy uses a sensitizing agent as part of an intentional medical treatment. Do not assume a medicine or photodynamic-treatment history automatically determines the safety of an aesthetic session using a different light source.

What to do before your next session

  1. Contact the clinic before attending. Share the medicine or supplement name, dose, start or change date and, if known, why it was prescribed.
  2. Report any recent reaction to sun or light, such as unusual redness, burning, swelling or blisters. This does not identify the cause, but helps the team assess your current skin.
  3. Ask which device and applicator are planned, for what purpose, and whether the current manual or instructions mention that medicine or condition.
  4. Confirm whether the session should proceed, wait or be reassessed. A test spot should only be done if the practitioner considers it appropriate for that device and situation.

Do not stop antibiotics, retinoids, heart medicines or any other prescribed treatment to “clear” an aesthetic procedure. If the session needs to be postponed, the prescribing team should guide any medication change. This page cannot give one waiting period for all medicines.

If a session has already taken place

If you started a new medicine after your appointment, tell the clinic and follow the instructions you were given. If you experience unexpected pain, worsening redness, blisters or persistent color change, contact the service that performed the treatment; severe or progressive symptoms may need medical assessment. Do not try to treat a reaction yourself or stop a medicine without guidance.

Questions to bring to the assessment

  • Is the medicine I started known to increase light sensitivity? Does that depend on dose, route or duration?
  • Does the planned source and applicator emit light relevant to this concern, or does its manual describe a specific precaution?
  • Does a medication change call for a new skin assessment or a test spot? How will that be decided?
  • Who should I contact if my skin reacts after the session?

Bring an up-to-date list of medicines and products. Assessment should consider the exact device and cannot be replaced by a generic internet list.

Sources and limitations

  • Review of complications from laser and energy-based procedures: discusses photosensitizing medicines and the lack of a standardized drug list or discontinuation period.
  • ARPANSA: Australian provider advice for laser, IPL and LED; includes reassessment when a potentially photosensitizing medicine is started.

These sources do not identify the local Harmony configuration or determine individual eligibility, Brazilian contraindications or when treatment may resume. Confirm the device, manual and plan with the practitioner.
