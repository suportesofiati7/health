Blackheads or milia: why extraction needs assessment

Blackheads and milia can look like white or dark bumps, but they are not necessarily the same condition. Learn why distinguishing them changes the discussion about extraction.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Similar appearances can be confusing

Not every pale bump is a closed comedone

Open comedones, closed comedones and small pale bumps may be described similarly in a search or photograph. Appearance and location alone cannot confirm what a lesion is. Before a cleansing appointment, the useful question is not only “can it be extracted?” but “has someone assessed whether this is a comedone and whether extraction makes sense?”.

02 · Milia have distinct characteristics

Milia and comedones are not interchangeable terms

A milium is a small keratin-filled cyst and can be mistaken for a whitehead. The Australasian College of Dermatologists and the American Academy of Ophthalmology advise against squeezing milia at home; trying can injure or scar the skin. A health professional can distinguish milia from comedones and other changes when appearance or persistence calls for assessment. Not every bump is a target for a facial cleansing.

03 · Do not try to identify a bump by squeezing it

Acne extraction is not for every lesion

The American Academy of Dermatology describes acne extraction as a professional technique using sterile instruments to remove acne blackheads and whiteheads in selected situations. This does not mean every pale lesion is acne, that extraction is always appropriate or that a cosmetic cleansing service replaces acne diagnosis and treatment. Avoid needles, tweezers and repeated pressure at home; these can injure the skin and increase irritation or scarring.

04 · Assessment may change the next step

What to ask before a cleansing appointment

Before an appointment, share how long the bumps have been present, whether they have changed, hurt or become inflamed, and what you have tried. Ask whether the change looks like a comedone or needs clinical assessment, which steps are planned and when the practitioner would avoid extraction. Read about the deep skin-cleansing service and the guide to blackheads and safe extraction. Reading cannot identify an individual lesion; persistent, new or sensitive-area changes should be assessed by a health professional.
