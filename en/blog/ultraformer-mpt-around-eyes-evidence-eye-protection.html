Ultraformer MPT around the eyes: what should you ask about evidence and protection?

The eye area is a distinct treatment question

“Around the eyes” can refer to different areas, including brow, eyelid, crow’s feet, and infraorbital skin. They are close to the globe and have delicate anatomy, so a general face-treatment description does not establish that periocular treatment is offered, appropriate, or performed with a particular safety method.

Before deciding, ask which exact area is proposed, which handpiece and current instructions apply, who performs the procedure, and how the eyes are protected. Do not infer the clinic’s eye-area protocol from a study or from the Ultraformer product name.

What the Ultraformer MPT eyelid study observed

A prospective cohort study included 34 Korean women aged 31–67 with mild-to-moderate eyelid laxity. It assessed one periocular treatment using a specific 2.0-mm, 4-MHz probe and followed participants for 12 weeks. Corneal shields were used in both eyes. Average upper-eyelid length decreased by about 0.94 mm; 76% of participants reported visible improvement, which blinded clinicians recognized in 59% of cases. Mild pain and temporary redness or swelling were reported. One author was affiliated with the manufacturer; the article declared no conflicts of interest.

This was a small, single-arm study in a selected population with one device configuration and short follow-up. It cannot predict an individual result, establish the clinic’s protocol, or rule out rare eye injury.

What broader evidence and case reports add

A 2026 systematic review identified ten studies of focused-ultrasound technologies around the eyes. The devices, treatment areas, methods, and protection reporting varied; the reviewers rated certainty as very low because most evidence was nonrandomized and heterogeneous. The review is useful for seeing both the limited evidence base and the differences among platforms.

Published case reports describe ocular injuries after cosmetic focused ultrasound near the eyes, including a 2026 report of bilateral injury after periocular treatment with a different HIFU device. Case reports cannot estimate how often an injury occurs or establish the risk for Ultraformer MPT. They do support asking about trained operators, device-specific instructions, ocular protection, and a plan if eye symptoms occur.

Questions to ask before consenting

  • Which specific area is being treated: brow, eyelid, crow’s feet, or under-eye skin?
  • Is periocular treatment included in the current instructions for the exact device and handpiece at this clinic?
  • What eye-protection method is planned, and who is responsible for placing and checking it?
  • What symptoms during or after treatment should prompt immediate contact or eye assessment?
  • Would another option or deciding not to treat near the eye better fit my concern?

Seek prompt eye care for new eye pain, redness, tearing, light sensitivity, or blurred vision after a periocular procedure. Do not wait for an aesthetic follow-up if vision changes.

Sources and limits

  • Hwang et al. (2026), prospective cohort study of Ultraformer MPT for upper and lower eyelid laxity.
  • 2026 systematic review of focused ultrasound for periorbital rejuvenation; ten heterogeneous studies, very-low-certainty evidence.
  • Ho et al. (2026), case report of bilateral ocular injury after periocular cosmetic HIFU using a different device.

These sources do not identify the exact handpiece, safety method, or eye-area service offered locally. Confirm the current manual and clinic protocol with the practitioner.
