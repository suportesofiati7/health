Monopolar or bipolar radiofrequency: what the terms explain

Monopolar and bipolar describe electrode configurations, but do not by themselves define a device’s intensity, outcome or protocol. Learn what to ask.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Monopolar and bipolar refer to electrode configuration

The terms describe how electrodes are arranged

In general terms, “monopolar” and “bipolar” describe different electrode configurations and current pathways in a system. These technical classifications help identify device families. By themselves, they do not provide every detail about frequency, cooling, treatment area, application mode or clinical goal.

02 · Neither label guarantees depth or effect

Configuration is not a complete comparison of outcomes

It is not accurate to conclude from the label alone that one configuration is always deeper, stronger, safer or more effective. Each device’s design, applicator, controls, area and assessment affect what a study or manufacturer describes. “Multipolar” also does not explain how a specific system works without its documentation.

03 · Reviews show variation across studies

Studies include different devices, areas and plans

A 2022 systematic review retained 23 clinical articles on monopolar and bipolar radiofrequency after reviewing more than 200 records; studies varied in area, device, treatment count and follow-up. This illustrates why a conclusion about a category does not automatically predict results from another device or for an individual. The literature does not identify the device available at this clinic.

04 · Turn the classification into useful questions

Ask about the device and specific proposal

Ask which configuration, model and applicator are being considered, for what goal and area; which evidence applies to that system; how risks and recovery will be explained; and which alternatives exist. The radiofrequency page does not specify a model. For a balanced technology comparison, also read focused ultrasound or radiofrequency.
