Ultrasonic peeling and blackhead extraction: are they the same step?

The ultrasonic spatula described by the service and comedone extraction are different procedures. Learn what to confirm about the clinic’s protocol.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; confirm the local protocol

01 · Similar names can describe different steps

Cleansing or exfoliation does not automatically mean extraction

The ultrasonic-peeling service page describes a vibrating spatula used on moistened skin to assist with surface residue and superficial cells. That describes the tool and purpose presented there; by itself, it does not confirm that blackheads will be extracted during the appointment.

Comedone extraction is a distinct step: the American Academy of Dermatology describes it as physically removing blackheads and whiteheads with sterile instruments, performed by a dermatologist. This clarifies the term “extraction”; it does not define a clinic’s protocol or professional scope in Brazil.

02 · What the service page confirms—and what it leaves open

Do not assume two techniques are included in one appointment

The local public description mentions a metal spatula with ultrasonic vibration used on moistened skin and lists surface residue and mild uneven texture as concerns. It does not say whether manual comedone extraction is included, what criteria determine that step, or which spatula model is used.

This does not prove that extraction never occurs; it means the available information does not confirm it as part of this service. The device name alone also cannot show whether it removes a comedone, treats acne or replaces dermatology assessment.

03 · When blackheads or acne are the main concern

Identify the concern before choosing a step

Blackheads and whiteheads are comedones, but not every lesion or texture described as a “clogged pore” is a comedone suitable for extraction. Inflamed, painful, widespread or persistent acne may need dermatology assessment and care; cosmetic cleansing should not be presented as a substitute for acne treatment.

If comedones are your main concern, say so during assessment and ask who will examine the skin, which technique is planned and when care should be postponed or referred. Do not try to reproduce professional extraction techniques at home.

04 · Questions that clarify the protocol

Confirm each step before deciding

Ask: does the appointment include only the ultrasonic spatula, or manual extraction as well? If extraction is included, who performs it and what criteria are used? What happens if the skin is inflamed or sensitised? What is the device model and its manufacturer-stated intended use? What outcome does this service not promise?

The answer may be that extraction is not part of the service, depends on assessment, or that another form of care is more appropriate. The goal is to understand the actual appointment without treating “ultrasonic peeling”, deep cleansing and acne as interchangeable terms.

05 · References and limits

References and limits

AAD guidance describes extraction in the US dermatology context. It does not confirm that this clinic offers that step, define Brazilian professional authorisation or establish the effect of the local ultrasonic device.
