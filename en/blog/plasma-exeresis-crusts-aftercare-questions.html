Crusts after plasma exeresis: aftercare questions to ask

Spots or crusts should not be assumed for every technology called plasma. The modality, device, treatment area and depth affect aftercare.

“Plasma” is not an aftercare instruction

Plasma exeresis, cold plasma and plasma skin-regeneration systems are not interchangeable terms. Some thermal procedures may leave dots or crusts; other protocols may look different and heal differently. The word “plasma” alone does not tell you what should happen to the skin or which products to use.

The service page in Londrina describes superficial plasma technology but does not publicly identify the manufacturer, model or modality. It is therefore not possible to confirm whether crusting is expected for the service offered or give a timeline for it to resolve.

What specific studies can tell us

A descriptive study of plasma exeresis in dermatology reported variation in how long pink redness lasted after crusts detached and noted that aftercare, general health, area mobility and age may be related factors. Its design did not compare aftercare routines or establish a universal rule.

DermNet’s clinical guidance for the Portrait plasma skin-regeneration system advises against picking or peeling skin after higher-energy protocols because of scarring concerns. This is specific to that system and does not define instructions for the unidentified local device. These sources show why the exact procedure must be confirmed.

Confirm these points with the team before leaving

  • The modality, device manufacturer and model, and the area treated.
  • Whether spots, crusts or flaking are expected for that protocol and which changes should be reported.
  • How to cleanse and protect the area, and which products to pause or avoid — with a timeframe set by the team.
  • Whether makeup, sun exposure, exercise, swimming or other procedures need to be restricted for that area.
  • When follow-up is planned, which contact channel to use and who will assess a concern.

Do not pull off crusts or start ointments, antiseptics, antibiotics, acids or home remedies on your own. Generic wound advice may not fit the procedure performed; follow the written plan from the team that knows the device and settings.

When to ask for an assessment

Contact the team if pain or redness is increasing, the area is getting worse, there is discharge, extensive blistering, fever or a major color change. These signs cannot be diagnosed remotely and may need clinical assessment. Seek urgent care in an emergency.

As general wound information — not a plasma protocol — the American Academy of Dermatology advises watching for worsening pain, redness, swelling or discharge in skin wounds and seeking guidance if signs of infection appear.

References and limits

  • Descriptive study of plasma exeresis applications in dermatology — observational series; it does not establish universal aftercare.
  • DermNet: plasma skin regeneration (Portrait) — describes care and recovery for that system; it does not identify the local modality.
  • American Academy of Dermatology: wound care to minimize scars — general wound guidance, not plasma-specific instructions.

The clinic’s modality, device, indication and aftercare need confirmation by the responsible practitioner before being presented as a local protocol.
