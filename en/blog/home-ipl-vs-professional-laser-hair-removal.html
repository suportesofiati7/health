At-home IPL or professional laser: how to compare the technologies

At-home IPL devices and professional lasers are not the same technology and do not necessarily share the same indications. Learn what to check before deciding.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Different light sources

IPL and laser are not interchangeable terms

Intense pulsed light (IPL) emits a range of wavelengths; a laser uses a narrower band of light. The terms are not interchangeable, and neither one alone describes a device’s output, safety mechanism, intended use or performance. “At-home laser hair removal” can also be imprecise marketing: check whether the manual identifies IPL or laser and what use the manufacturer states.

02 · Device and intended use must match

“Home use” does not answer every question

A consumer device has its own instructions and limits, which vary by model. Registration in another country does not establish its regulatory status in Brazil or authorise anyone to use it on any area. Brazil’s health authority advises checking device regularisation and the manufacturer’s instructions about qualified operators. Do not judge safety from advertising, customer reviews or a seal without checking the exact product.

03 · Safety depends on the device and the person

Compare intended use, instructions and limits

Skin and hair characteristics, body area, recent sun exposure, medicines and a history of reactions may change the safety discussion. Eye protection, sensors, skin-tone limits and patch-test instructions vary by device. Do not use a device contrary to its instructions, over a tattoo or irritated skin, or change frequency or intensity on your own. If you experience a burn, blistering, severe pain or persistent skin changes, stop and seek appropriate health advice.

04 · Ask before combining or switching methods

What to bring to a professional assessment

Before choosing or switching methods, note the brand and model, technology listed in the manual, intended area, skin tone, hair colour, recent use and prior reactions. Ask whether the technology and intended use match your goal, which limits and risks apply, and which professional is qualified to perform the procedure.
