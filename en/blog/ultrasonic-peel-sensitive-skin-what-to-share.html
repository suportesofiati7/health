Sensitive skin before ultrasonic peeling: what should you share?

Burning, itching, redness and dryness are useful details to share before a procedure. Learn what to report and ask without self-diagnosing.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational guide; confirm local protocol

Describe what you feel and when it began

Tell the practitioner if your skin burns, stings, itches, becomes red or swollen, feels very dry or tender — and when this started. Explain whether it happens after washing, using a product or without a clear trigger. These details help the practitioner understand the situation, but they do not identify the cause by themselves.

If possible, bring the names or photos of products you use on your face and note recent changes. Skin that reacts to cosmetics may need a different assessment from skin without current irritation.

Why this matters before ultrasonic peeling

The service page describes using an ultrasonic spatula but does not identify the manufacturer, model, tip or device instructions. Without those details, it is not possible to say how the device should be used for every skin condition or publish a universal safety rule.

Tell the practitioner about current burning or itching, persistent redness, peeling, open areas, allergies or diagnosed skin conditions. Ask whether your skin should be assessed first, whether the proposed procedure is still appropriate, or whether dermatology guidance is needed. A webpage cannot decide this for you.

Bring your product and procedure history

List recent cosmetics and topical medicines, especially products that caused a reaction, along with procedures on the area and approximate dates. If you take a prescribed medicine, share its name and reason; do not stop or change treatment for a cosmetic procedure without discussing it with the prescriber.

Do not try to “prepare” your skin with extra exfoliation, home remedies or a new product. If you are unsure what to use before your appointment, ask the practitioner responsible.

Questions for a clear next step

What is the device model and where can I read the manufacturer’s instructions? How will you assess skin that is burning, itching or peeling? Does the procedure’s goal fit what my skin needs now? Is there a reason to postpone or seek dermatology advice? What written care instructions should I follow?

Answers should reflect the actual device, your history and an in-person assessment. This article does not define contraindications or replace the local protocol, which should be explained before treatment.
