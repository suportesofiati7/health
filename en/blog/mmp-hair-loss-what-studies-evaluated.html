MMP for hair loss: what did the studies actually evaluate?

“Microinfusion” alone does not identify the medicine or the evidence. See what MMP studies assessed—and why reviews of other injectable therapies do not establish a clinic’s protocol.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

This page interprets published studies to help you ask informed questions. It does not diagnose alopecia, recommend a procedure or assume which medicine, device or protocol the practitioner uses.

01 · The name does not describe the whole intervention

MMP describes a delivery technique, not one universal formula

Articles describe MMP as medication microinfusion into the skin using tattoo equipment, creating microchannels and depositing substances. What a study evaluates also depends on the medicine, indication, technique and study design. A result tied to one specific combination therefore does not prove that “MMP” works for every type of hair loss or identify the product used in a clinic.

02 · MMP-specific literature began with a small evidence base

A 2023 Brazilian publication described only a few case reports

A Brazilian editorial review of scalp microinfusion noted that, despite growing use, the literature on hair disorders was limited to a few case reports. Its examples included reports of androgenetic alopecia and alopecia areata using different substances and in different contexts. Reports without a comparison group can raise hypotheses, but cannot reliably measure benefit, compare options or predict typical outcomes.

03 · Newer reviews expand the literature without making it uniform

A 2026 narrative review included different techniques and hair disorders

A narrative review published in 2026 identified 14 studies of MMP as a delivery technique; eight addressed alopecias, including different conditions. It arranged the work chronologically and summarized varied applications. This broader map is not the same as a comparative clinical trial and does not provide one overall estimate of efficacy. Findings in alopecia areata, androgenetic alopecia and scarring alopecias cannot be treated as interchangeable.

04 · “Injectable therapy” is a broader category

A review of 30 androgenetic alopecia studies is not a review of MMP alone

A 2026 systematic review of injectable therapies for androgenetic alopecia included 30 reports: seven interventional, 11 observational and 12 descriptive. Agents, delivery methods and schedules varied. The authors noted small samples, heterogeneous protocols and short follow-up; adverse events included paradoxical alopecia, scarring and acute dermatitis. These findings provide context for injectable therapies as a category, but cannot be presented as MMP-specific results.

05 · Compare each study with the proposal

Four matches help show whether the evidence answers the question

  • Is the diagnosis or hair-loss pattern being studied the same one under assessment?
  • Are the substance, route, concentration and purpose the same?
  • Did the study assess MMP with a medicine, microneedling without a medicine, or another injection method?
  • Was there a comparison group, sufficient follow-up and a defined outcome measure?

When these features differ, a study may offer context but does not show that a local proposal will have the same effect.

06 · What still needs confirmation

Protocol, medicine and indication cannot be inferred from the term MMP

Before comparing studies, ask which condition is being assessed, which substance and regulatory category would be used, which device and route are planned, and which alternatives were considered. The practitioner must confirm the actual details and their suitability. This page does not provide a concentration, dose, frequency, depth, eligibility decision or hair-growth promise.

07 · References and limits

Sources reviewed

  • Cortez de Almeida, Melo and Tosti: Brazilian editorial review of scalp microinfusion (2023)
  • Nagshabandi et al.: narrative review of MMP as a delivery technique (2026)
  • Beer et al.: systematic review of injectable therapies for androgenetic alopecia (2026)

The papers use different definitions and treatment groups. The injectable-therapy review is not specific to MMP; the narrative review does not estimate a pooled effect. None of these sources identifies the substance, device, registration or protocol offered by Franciele.
