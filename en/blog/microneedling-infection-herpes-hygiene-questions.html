Microneedling: infection, herpes and hygiene questions

Questions about infection history, device hygiene and products to discuss before a professional microneedling assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Evidence-informed reading

This is a question guide, not a diagnosis, eligibility checklist or device-specific procedure manual. FDA information applies to the United States and does not establish the Brazilian status of the clinic’s device.

01 · The procedure crosses the skin barrier

Why hygiene and medical history belong in the consultation

Microneedling creates controlled punctures in the skin. That makes the condition of the treatment area, equipment preparation, materials and anything applied to the skin relevant. A careful consultation considers more than the cosmetic concern: it also asks about healing, infections and prior reactions. This page organizes questions; it does not determine who is eligible or describe how to perform the procedure.

02 · Share infection and herpes history

Current activity and prior episodes are useful information

Tell the practitioner about a wound, infection, significant irritation or painful lesions in the area, including cold sores or another recurring infection. Also mention prior herpes episodes in the area and any history of difficult healing or reactions after procedures. The AAD advises waiting until infection and inflamed acne have cleared; the FDA lists active infection and an active cold sore among situations to discuss for devices evaluated in the United States. Do not use an online list to decide that you are eligible or ineligible: assessment depends on the condition, device and responsible practitioner.

03 · Health history is not an automatic contraindication

The practitioner needs context about factors that can affect healing

Share conditions or medicines that affect immunity, healing or bleeding, allergies to metals or anesthetics, and any history of raised scars or keloids. Mention acne medicines and do not stop any medicine on your own. Sources and device instructions can differ on some factors and timing; the practitioner should review the actual device instructions, your history and advice from the prescriber. This page does not set medication pause periods or replace medical assessment.

04 · Ask about the cartridge and device cleaning

How to understand materials without assuming the model

Ask which device will be used, how the manufacturer instructs reusable components to be cleaned and disinfected, and whether the needle cartridge is new and single-patient for that appointment. The FDA advises patients to ask about a new cartridge and device cleaning. That guidance relates to devices evaluated in the United States; it does not replace the manual, Brazilian sanitary rules or confirmation of the clinic’s actual device.

05 · Confirm any product used with the technique

Topical use and use through microchannels are not the same

Ask for the product name, purpose, intended route and regulatory category. Anvisa explains that cosmetics are for external use; products intended to enter through microneedling should not be treated as topical-use cosmetics and need the appropriate medicine or medical-device regulatory category. Category and use must match the exact product and applicable instructions. This question does not assume that the clinic uses any particular product.

06 · Agree on aftercare and contact before leaving

Know whom to contact if the skin worsens

Ask for written aftercare instructions for the actual protocol, which changes are expected and which channel to use if the skin worsens or you have a concern. Do not apply medicines or substances on your own to treat a reaction. If the area has an infection, wound or active cold sore on the day, tell the practitioner before any procedure and wait for professional guidance.

07 · A short checklist for consultation

Questions that support an informed decision

Which device and cartridge are used? How is equipment cleaned between appointments? Is the cartridge disposed of according to the manufacturer’s instructions? Will any product be applied through the microchannels—what is its purpose and regulatory status? How do my herpes, infection, allergy or healing history affect assessment? Which signs call for contact, and who will be available? A responsible answer should identify the actual model and plan; generic explanations cannot confirm the local protocol.
