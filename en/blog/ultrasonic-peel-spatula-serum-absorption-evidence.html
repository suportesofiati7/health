Ultrasonic peeling: does a spatula increase serum absorption?

A technology name does not answer which handpiece, device or evidence applies. Use the consultation to confirm those details.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · The public description is a starting point

It does not replace identifying the device

The service page describes a spatula with ultrasonic vibration to help remove residue and superficial cells. This states the general public proposal, but does not identify the manufacturer, model, frequency, emitted power, mode of use or device instructions. “Ultrasonic peeling” is not a complete technical specification.

02 · Similar names do not prove the same technology

Do not transfer findings from brushes or other ultrasound systems

Literature on sonic brushes, therapeutic ultrasound, diagnostic imaging or other aesthetic devices does not automatically identify the spatula used here. Devices that use vibration or sound waves can still differ in construction, purpose and instructions. Findings for one device do not establish benefits or safety for another.

03 · Confirm the applicable documentation

Regulatory status depends on the product and its stated use

Ask for the manufacturer and model and review the matching documentation, including instructions in Portuguese and stated purpose. Anvisa provides a search for regulated health products; a search result or name match alone does not prove that the product, purpose or clinical indication is the same. The local model and classification need practitioner confirmation.

04 · Questions before deciding

Ask for answers tied to the actual device

  • What are the equipment manufacturer, model and manual?
  • What intended purpose does the manufacturer state for this handpiece?
  • Which frequency and output data are documented?
  • What evidence applies to this device and purpose?
  • How are the spatula and reusable items cleaned or processed?
