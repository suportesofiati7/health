Professional skin cleansing and acne: different roles

Professional cleansing and extraction may address some comedones, but they are not the same as an acne care plan. Learn the limits and when to seek assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Cleansing is not a complete acne plan

Cleansing and acne care answer different questions

A professional cleansing session may have a focused goal, such as cosmetic surface care or addressing some comedones, depending on assessment and the service actually offered. Acne, however, has different presentations and levels of severity. A cleansing session should not be presented as an automatic substitute for appropriate assessment and treatment when lesions are inflamed, persistent, painful or leave marks.

02 · Lesion type and severity matter

Comedones are only part of the picture

Dark or light spots may be comedones, but other changes can look similar. Number, inflammation, pain, recurrence and marks all affect the discussion. Dermatology guidelines address acne through specific, individualised approaches; procedures such as extraction may have a limited role in some cases, but cannot by themselves address every cause or prevent new lesions.

03 · Squeezing can damage the skin

Extraction has limits and requires care

Squeezing or manipulating lesions at home can worsen inflammation and increase the risk of marks. Extraction should not be promised as part of every cleansing session: deciding whether a lesion can be addressed requires identifying its type, the skin’s condition and professional limits. If acne is widespread, painful nodules or scarring are present, irritation is significant, or the diagnosis is unclear, seek assessment from an appropriately qualified acne-care professional before choosing a cosmetic procedure.

04 · A useful conversation starts with concerns and history

How to choose the next step

At an appointment, describe how long lesions have appeared, whether they hurt or leave marks, which products or treatments you have tried, and whether your skin is irritated. Ask what cleansing can realistically address, what falls outside its scope, and when medical assessment is appropriate. Also read about professional deep cleansing and procedures when acne is inflamed. This information does not diagnose or prescribe treatment.
