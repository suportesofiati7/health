Fractional and ablative CO₂ laser: what the terms mean

Learn how ablative and fractional CO₂ laser terms differ, what they do not tell you, and what to ask during an assessment.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Terms answer different questions

Ablative and fractional describe different things

“Ablative” describes an effect on tissue: energy removes or vaporizes part of the skin’s surface layers. “Fractional” describes how energy is delivered: small treated columns are separated by areas of untreated skin. A laser can be ablative and fractional at the same time. So “fractional” does not automatically mean non-ablative or mild.

02 · Energy distribution matters

What changes when treatment is fractional

With full-field treatment, the target area is treated more continuously; with fractional treatment, microscopic treated zones alternate with spared tissue. This can affect healing and the pattern of side effects, but it cannot predict recovery by itself. Intensity, density, passes, area, goal and skin characteristics also matter. There is no universal recovery calendar for every CO₂ laser treatment.

03 · A device name is not enough

The laser name does not reveal the full protocol

The term “CO₂” does not tell you which mode, handpiece, settings or treatment area will be used, or confirm which options a clinic offers. The NCBI review describes variation between fractional and full-field treatments; it does not identify Franciele’s device or determine what is suitable for an individual. Do not infer that every mode or indication is available from an equipment name.

04 · Bring questions, not a fixed expectation

Useful questions for an assessment

Before deciding, ask which treatment mode is planned and why; which area will be treated; what outcome is plausible; which alternatives exist; how your history of pigmentation, healing or sun exposure affects the discussion; what recovery may look like for that specific plan; and who will follow your progress.
