Radiofrequency and pigment history: what can studies tell us?

“Radiofrequency” describes a family of technologies, and pigmentation studies do not all assess the same device or goal. Share any history of darkening or lightening after irritation, acne or procedures; it helps frame questions but does not predict your result by itself.

Radiofrequency is not one modality

Monopolar, bipolar, fractional and microneedling radiofrequency differ in design, skin contact and energy delivery. A paper on microneedling radiofrequency does not establish an effect for a device without needles, and a monopolar study does not represent every platform. Ask the practitioner to identify the manufacturer, model, handpiece, intended use and indication being discussed for you.

What pigmentation studies assessed

A 2022 clinical study assessed pulsed-type microneedling radiofrequency in 44 people diagnosed with melasma or Riehl melanosis, using five sessions and follow-up through eight weeks after the last session. The authors reported improvement in some participants and no serious adverse events during the study period. This describes specific protocols and conditions; it does not establish that radiofrequency treats pigmentation in general or predict another person’s response or device.

Favorable results also have limits

A 2026 open pilot study assessed a dual-wave microneedling radiofrequency device for acne scars and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in seven people with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI; six completed it. No new hyperpigmentation was reported, but the small sample, lack of a control group and unblinded assessment limit the conclusion. A separate retrospective monopolar radiofrequency study in 26 Asian women assessed aging-associated pigmentation — a different population and goal.

How to use this information

These studies show why a generic technology name is not enough. The diagnosis of the color change, modality, device, treatment area and selected outcome all change the question. A history of pigment change may be important to disclose, but these findings do not provide a universal rule that someone is suitable or unsuitable. If a mark is new, changing or not yet assessed, speak with a qualified professional before treating it as a cosmetic target.

Questions for an assessment

  • Is the proposed device microneedling or non-needling? What are its manufacturer, model and handpiece?
  • What is the goal for my concern, and what evidence used this modality?
  • Did the cited studies assess a mark like mine or a different condition?
  • How would my history of darkening, lightening or scarring affect the plan?
  • What changes should I report, and whom should I contact if they occur?
Do not start, stop or change medicines or products to prepare your skin without individual guidance.

Sources and limits

  • Pulsed-type microneedling radiofrequency study in melasma and Riehl melanosis (44 participants).
  • Dual-wave microneedling radiofrequency pilot for acne scars and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (six completers).
  • Retrospective monopolar radiofrequency study of aging-associated pigmentation (26 women).
The studies assessed different devices, populations and goals. None identifies the clinic’s device or protocol; confirm those details and applicable device registration with the practitioner.
