CO₂ laser and skin tone: how to discuss pigmentation risk

Phototype, a history of pigment changes and laser modality all inform assessment. Learn what CO₂ studies can—and cannot—tell you.

By Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational clinical reading

01 · Pigment changes belong in the discussion

Why discuss pigmentation before deciding

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) is a colour change that can follow skin inflammation or injury. A review focused on CO₂ laser describes PIH as a relevant adverse effect in part of the literature. This does not mean it occurs in every case or that a web page can predict an individual response. It belongs in a discussion of treatment goals, delivery mode and expected recovery.

02 · The review did not find a universal answer

What a review of studies found

A 2023 review identified 211 records and included 14 relevant studies. Skin type IV was most common among participants, followed by type III; the authors found that PIH incidence varied across studies and did not find a conclusive influence of skin phenotype. They noted a lack of sufficiently powered clinical studies to clarify risk factors. The findings therefore do not support a simple rule that a particular skin tone is always safe or always unsuitable.

03 · Modality and protocol still matter

What remains uncertain for each person

“CO₂ laser” covers different modes and protocols; findings from one study do not automatically transfer to another device, technique, treatment area or person. A history of marks after inflammation, sun exposure, current skin condition, goal, proposed modality and ability to follow instructions deserve individual discussion. The review also does not define which products should be used before or after treatment or validate a clinic protocol. Do not start medicines or creams on your own.

04 · Bring your history to the consultation

Questions for an informed assessment

Ask which modality and goal are being considered; how your history of pigmentation and healing affects the discussion; which pigment changes may occur; what alternatives exist; how follow-up will work; and which signs require contact. For context, also read about fractional and ablative terminology and recovery planning. Suitability depends on assessment and the actual device and protocol used.
