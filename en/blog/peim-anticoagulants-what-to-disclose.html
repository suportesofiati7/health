PEIM and anticoagulants: what should you disclose?

Taking an anticoagulant or antiplatelet? Share its name, dose and reason for use before planning any procedure. Do not stop or change a medicine on your own.

Before scheduling

Bring an up-to-date list

  1. Medicine, dose, reason and prescriber.
  2. History of clots, bleeding or allergy.
  3. Confirm who decides on any change.

Disclose every medicine — even those that seem unrelated

Tell the team about anticoagulants (for example, warfarin or direct oral anticoagulants), antiplatelet medicines (such as aspirin or clopidogrel), hormones, supplements and nonprescription medicines. Include why you take each one, who prescribed it and any recent changes. Bring a current list or the packages if that helps confirm the names.

Also disclose a personal history of blood clots, pulmonary embolism, bleeding disorders, unusual bleeding, substance allergies and any current leg symptoms. These details help the team decide whether the indication needs clarification or an elective decision should be deferred.

Sclerotherapy instructions are not uniform

NHS hospital information advises patients to tell their doctor about anticoagulants before sclerotherapy, but the materials describe different approaches: some say a pause may be needed in specific situations; others advise continuing unless the responsible medical team explicitly says otherwise. This variation is why there is no safe rule for patients to apply on their own.

Venous guidelines discuss sclerotherapy for telangiectasias and reticular veins and emphasize assessment of individual risks and contraindications. However, recommendations for medical sclerotherapy — often guided by a diagnosis, substance and technique of its own — do not automatically establish the safety or protocol of this clinic’s PEIM.

Do not stop an anticoagulant, aspirin or another medicine on your own

Stopping a prescribed medicine can carry risks related to the condition for which it was prescribed; continuing or pausing it also depends on the specific procedure and your history. Do not change doses, skip doses, or stop hormones or supplements to “prepare your skin” without a coordinated clinical decision. If the team assessing the procedure thinks a change should be discussed, they should guide you to contact the prescriber and clarify who is responsible for that decision.

If there is not enough time for that discussion before the planned date, postpone the elective procedure until the plan is clear. An information page cannot assess an individual’s bleeding or clotting risk.

Useful questions for your appointment

  • Which substance and technique are being considered, and what is the procedure’s purpose?
  • What is known about my medicine for this specific technique?
  • Should I speak with the prescriber before deciding? Who will coordinate that conversation?
  • If the procedure is not appropriate now, what alternatives or the option of no intervention make sense?
  • Which signs afterward would require contact or urgent assessment, and which channel should I use?

If you develop sudden pain or swelling in one leg, shortness of breath, chest pain or significant bleeding, seek urgent medical care instead of trying to solve it by changing medicine or waiting for an aesthetics appointment.

References and limits

  • Cambridge University Hospitals NHS: ultrasound-guided foam for varicose veins — advises disclosure of anticoagulants and describes any pause as uncommon and individualized.
  • Oxford University Hospitals NHS: ultrasound-guided sclerotherapy — varicose-vein-specific leaflet advising not to stop anticoagulants unless the medical team instructs you.
  • 2023 SVS/AVF/AVLS guideline for lower-extremity venous disease, part II — addresses telangiectasias and reticular veins in a vascular-care context; it does not define local PEIM.

The local service’s substance, technique, indication, professional scope and medication guidance are not specified here and need confirmation by the responsible practitioner. This guide does not replace advice from your prescriber or medical assessment.
