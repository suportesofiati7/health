What does each published description say?

The ultrasonic peel service describes a vibrating ultrasonic spatula to help remove surface residue and cells, as a light cleansing and exfoliation option. The deep-cleansing page describes cleansing, emollience and manual or instrumental extraction when indicated. These are the current service descriptions, not a direct clinical comparison of outcomes.

So the first conversation may be about surface cleansing or exfoliation; the second may include an assessment of comedones and whether selective extraction is appropriate. “Deep” does not mean every lesion will be extracted, and “ultrasonic” does not mean the spatula replaces an extraction step.

Extraction is a separate decision

The American Academy of Dermatology describes acne extraction as a selective option for persistent blackheads and whiteheads, performed with appropriate technique and instruments. This does not mean that the local deep-cleansing service includes extraction at every appointment: its own description says “when indicated.” Nor does it confirm that the ultrasonic peel includes that step.

Inflamed, painful or broken lesions, or spots whose diagnosis is uncertain, may need a different assessment. Do not try to reproduce extractions at home or assume that a technique treats acne, pigmentation or scars because the surface feels smoother afterward.

There is no direct evidence that one local protocol is superior

The public pages do not specify the ultrasonic spatula’s manufacturer or model, operating parameters, associated products or extraction criteria. They also do not present a trial comparing these two services in the same people for the same goal. Therefore, it is not possible to say that one is better, more complete or safer for everyone.

Studies of other spatulas, sonophoresis, microdermabrasion or dermatologic extraction do not automatically identify this clinic’s device or protocol. Confirm the method during your assessment.

Questions that help you compare

  • Is my priority the feeling of cleanliness, surface texture or assessment of blackheads?
  • Exactly what will be done during the session, and which steps are optional?
  • Is extraction planned? If so, which lesions might be considered and which would not?
  • Which device and products would be used, and what manufacturer information guides that choice?
  • How do sensitivity, inflamed acne, marks after irritation or recent skin care change the decision?
  • Does expected recovery depend on any additional step?

Ask for the recommendation to be connected to your concern, skin assessment and device instructions. If the plan combines techniques, confirm the rationale and aftercare for each one separately.
