Plasma technology: why assess a skin lesion before treatment?

A skin lesion should be assessed before a destructive procedure. Learn what to ask about diagnosis, the device and its intended use.

Franciele Sofiati · CRBM 6277Educational information; medical assessment before treating a changing lesion

01 · A device is not a diagnosis

A device is not a diagnosis

A raised bump, a dark mark and a changing mole are not interchangeable concerns. Appearance in a photograph cannot establish the diagnosis. Aesthetic treatment should not be used to find out what a lesion is.

If a spot is new, changing, bleeding, crusting, painful or not healing, pause plans to treat it with an energy device and seek assessment from an appropriate medical professional, such as a dermatologist. These signs do not prove cancer; they are reasons to clarify the cause first.

02 · Diagnosis comes before destruction

Diagnosis comes before destruction

Some plasma devices create a controlled tissue effect. The exact effect depends on the modality and device, which are not identified on this clinic service page. A procedure that alters or removes a lesion may change its appearance and may not provide the tissue needed for laboratory diagnosis.

The American Academy of Dermatology explains that a biopsy is used when a dermatologist sees warning signs and that laboratory examination is needed to diagnose skin cancer. Not every spot needs biopsy; deciding whether it does belongs to the medical assessment.

03 · Identify the actual device and intended use

Identify the actual device and intended use

Before considering a procedure, ask for the manufacturer, exact model and physical modality; what condition and body area the manufacturer’s instructions cover; what the relevant regulatory status is; and whether the proposed use falls within the operator’s professional scope.

A device listing or registration does not by itself diagnose a lesion, prove benefit for every indication or confirm that a specific procedure is appropriate. Evidence about cold atmospheric plasma, plasma exeresis or another platform cannot be transferred automatically to a different device.

04 · Questions to take to an assessment

Questions to take to an assessment

  • Has this lesion been assessed medically, and is its diagnosis clear?
  • Does it need dermoscopy, biopsy or another medical assessment before a cosmetic procedure?
  • What exact device and intended use are proposed?
  • What are the alternatives, risks, aftercare and plan if the lesion changes?
  • Who should I contact if healing is not as expected?

Do not scrape, burn, puncture or try to remove a changing lesion at home. Bring a record of when it appeared and any changes you noticed. A photograph can help describe change, but it cannot replace an examination.
